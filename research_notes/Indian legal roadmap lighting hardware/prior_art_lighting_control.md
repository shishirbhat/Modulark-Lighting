## Key Question 1 — Prior art for the CALIBRATION SWEEP / LIGHT TRANSPORT MATRIX claim

### Takeaway
This is **not novel in any form**. "Energise each source individually, measure its contribution at sensor(s), assemble a contribution/transport matrix, then solve a constrained least-squares problem for target levels with daylight as a measured additive disturbance" is a textbook method with a 1993 computer-graphics origin, a 2010s building-lighting research literature, and granted patents from Philips/Signify, Enlighted (Siemens) and GE/Current that describe the individual-source sweep explicitly. The founder would be filing directly over identifiable art.

### Cited Findings

**Academic / non-patent prior art (a paper defeats novelty exactly as a patent does):**
- Schoeneman, Dorsey, Smits, Arvo & Greenberg, **"Painting With Light", SIGGRAPH '93, Proc. 20th Annual Conf. on Computer Graphics and Interactive Techniques, pp. 143–146 (1993)**. Stated approach: "determining light settings for an environment from a description of the desired solution … using a **constrained least squares approach** to match light intensities and colors to a target image." This is literally the claimed algorithm (targets → constrained least squares over per-source contributions), published 1993 — [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/166117.166135); [PDF/RG copy](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/220720989_Painting_With_Light)
- Afshari, Mishra, Julius, Lizarralde, Wason & Wen, **"Modeling and control of color tunable lighting systems", Energy and Buildings 68:242–253 (2014)**. Explicitly: "Measured input–output data may be used to identify the (static) **light transport matrix, P**. The typical approach is a **linear least squares fit** to input–output data … the least squares estimate of P calculated using the **Moore–Penrose pseudo-inverse** of the input matrix." The term "light transport matrix" applied to a luminaire→photosensor lighting system is therefore in the published literature by 2014 — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0378778813005331)
- Jia, Afshari, Mishra & Radke, **"Simulation for pre-visualizing and tuning lighting controller behavior", Energy and Buildings 70:287–302 (2014)** — same RPI Smart Lighting ERC group, same transport-matrix framing — [ref via Springer chapter](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-319-68462-8_9)
- Caicedo & Pandharipande (Philips Research), **"Distributed illumination control with local sensing and actuation in networked lighting systems", IEEE Sensors Journal (2013)**; and Pandharipande & Caicedo, **"Adaptive illumination rendering in LED lighting systems", IEEE Trans. on Systems, Man and Cybernetics: Systems (2013)** — distributed luminaires each with light sensors; controllers "determine optimum dimming levels under illumination constraints specified in terms of light sensor set-points" — [dblp: David Caicedo](https://dblp.org/pid/123/2665.html)

### Inferences
- Every element of item 1 — per-source excitation, contribution model, constrained least squares, daylight subtracted as an uncontrollable additive input — is individually and in combination disclosed. US9439260B2 (Philips/Signify, Pandharipande/Caicedo) plus the Afshari/Mishra 2014 transport-matrix paper together read on the claim as stated.
- Because Indian novelty under s.2(1)(l)/s.13 is absolute and worldwide, the 1993 SIGGRAPH paper alone is citable against the "constrained least squares to a target" element.
- The only conceivably patentable residue is a *very* specific implementation detail (e.g. a particular sweep schedule that is imperceptible to occupants, a specific regularisation, or a specific way of handling third-party luminaires with unknown dimming curves) — and even then it is an obviousness fight, not a novelty one.

## Key Question 2 — TARGET-STATE / outcome-based control ("Read / Relax / Cook"); is "user picks outcome, system solves" novel?

### Takeaway
"User selects a named outcome and the system works out device settings" is **exactly what every scene system has done since the 1990s** and is baked into the lighting control standards. The narrower framing — "solve for a *measured* target room state using a sensor, rather than recall stored per-device values" — is also already disclosed (closed-loop daylight harvesting with a target set-point; Philips "automatically rendering a lighting atmosphere" from an abstract description; illuminance-contribution control to a target illuminance). Not novel.

### Cited Findings

**Stored-preset scenes (the weak reading) — standardised, not patentable:**
- **IEC 62386** is the international standard underlying DALI; scene recall is a core DALI command set. DALI-2 certification is run by the DALI Alliance; **D4i** was added as a DALI-2 extension in **November 2019** — [DALI Alliance, IEC 62386](https://www.dali-alliance.org/standards/IEC62386.html); [DALI Alliance technology page](https://www.dali-alliance.org/about-us/technology.html)
- **IEC/EN 62386-303** = "Particular requirements – Input devices – Occupancy sensor"; **-304** = light level sensors; **-305** = colour sensor (added later as a new part). Parts 301/302/303/304 published together — [DiiA news: IEC publishes Parts 301, 302, 303, 304](https://www.dali-alliance.org/news/11/iec-publishes-new-parts-301-302-303-and-304-of-dali-standard); [DiiA news: colour sensor as Part 305](https://www.dali-alliance.org/news/490/colour-sensor-specification-added-as-new-part-305-of-iec-62386); [EN 62386-303:2017 record](https://standards.iteh.ai/catalog/standards/clc/daf87e31-13ed-4467-afbe-4a17463e5c63/en-62386-303-2017)
- Matter↔DALI bridging with commissioning, dimming, colour control and sensors is already implemented in shipping open-source components — [Espressif Developer Portal, ESP DALI component (Aug 2026)](https://developer.espressif.com/blog/2026/08/dali-component-lighting-systems/)
- [DALI Wikipedia overview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_Addressable_Lighting_Interface); [DALI-2 Technical Guide for Systems Integrators, Ozuno TG-24-001](https://ozuno.com/wp-content/uploads/DALI-2-Technical-Guide-for-Systems-Integrators.pdf)

**Outcome/atmosphere-based control (the strong reading) — also disclosed:**
- **US8463408** and **US9420673 — "Light control system and method for automatically rendering a lighting atmosphere"** (Philips family). Indexed content: "advanced light control systems … **automatically render certain lighting atmospheres in a room from an abstract description (such as an XML file) which is automatically processed to generate control values or parameters for the lighting modules**"; "lighting atmospheres … a collection of lighting effects that harmoniously concur in space and time." That is: user/author specifies the *outcome*, system computes the device parameters — [ref](https://patents.google.com/patent/EP2392194B1/ja) (family context)
- Signify's own technical framing: "a **lighting scene specifies a colour for each spatial point within the environment**, and **if a luminaire outputs only half the desired output power, this can be detected by a light sensor which may provide feedback**" — i.e. sensor-verified rendering of a spatially-specified target, not preset recall — [WO2017029368A1, spatial light effects based on lamp location](https://patents.google.com/patent/WO2017029368A1/fr); [Signify IP blog, Smart Lighting](https://www.signify.com/global/our-company/intellectual-property/news/blogs/smart-lighting-creating-your-experience-was-never-so-easy)
- **US7550931B2 — "Controlled lighting methods and apparatus"** (Color Kinetics → Philips) — foundational controlled-lighting-output art
- **US20060038959 — "Adaptive and interactive scene illumination"** (publ. 23 Feb 2006) — [Justia record](https://patents.justia.com/patent/20060038959)
- **US10051707B2 — "Methods and apparatus for controlling lighting based on combination of inputs"** — [ref](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10051707B2/en)
- **US11363701 — "Method for controlling a lighting system using a lighting control console"** (granted 14 June 2022) — [Justia](https://patents.justia.com/patent/11363701)
- **US10485076** (above) selects luminaires by illuminance contribution and solves to a **target illuminance** — the "measured target room state" reading
- Enlighted/Siemens commissioning policy art: "Policies entered at the time of commissioning are to be combined with sensor readings at operating time to come up with multiple strategies (algorithms) for controlling power output to lighting devices. These algorithms **optimize light levels for efficiency, security, comfort, safety and other factors**" — outcome-goal driven solving — [US20120086363A1](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20120086363); [Enlighted product doc, Building Robotics Inc. 2022](https://files.orionlighting.com/resources/PRODUCT/Controls-Sensors/Enlighted%20Lighting%20Control.pdf)
- Third-party luminaire interop is Signify's explicit licensing business, not a defensible gap: the **EnabLED** programme licenses "fifty separate inventions … to make LED lamps compatible with a wide range of commercially available dimmers … utilized in third party dimmer compatible lamps" — [Signify EnabLED](https://www.signify.com/global/our-company/intellectual-property/news/blogs/mains-dimmable-lamps)

### Inferences
- The distinction the founder draws ("recall a stored device preset labelled Read" vs "solve for a measured target room state") is real as an engineering distinction but is **not a gap in the art**. Closed-loop daylight harvesting has solved to a measured target set-point since at least the 1990s; the Philips atmosphere-rendering family solves device parameters from an abstract outcome description; the illuminance-contribution family solves to a target illuminance across multiple luminaires.
- "Including third-party luminaires" adds nothing novel — DALI-2/D4i, Zigbee/ZLL, Matter and Bluetooth Mesh exist precisely to make multi-vendor luminaires addressable, and using standards-compliant devices is the expected use of those standards.
- At best there is a *narrow combination* claim: named human-legible outcome → per-room measured target vector → constrained solve over a commissioned contribution matrix including heterogeneous third-party sources. Each limb is in the art; the combination would face a strong obviousness objection.

### Gaps
- I could not locate a patent that claims *literally* "receive a semantic outcome label, map it to a measured room-state target vector, and solve over a commissioned transport matrix" as a single claim. Absence of a found reference is **not** evidence of novelty here given the blocked databases — a professional search on CPC classes **H05B47/105, H05B47/11, H05B47/115, H05B47/155, H05B47/165, H05B47/19** is required.
- Could not verify Zigbee Light Link / ZCL Scenes cluster specification dates from a primary source in this session.

## Key Question 3 — LEARNING FROM OVERRIDES ONLY, with measured daylight as a context feature

### Takeaway
Learning implicit preferences from manual overrides is heavily patented — Nest/Google for thermostats, ABL IP Holding (Acuity) and Signify for lighting, Apple for display brightness. The specific "measured outdoor/daylight level as a context-vector feature rather than a weather API" distinction is **not a differentiator**: closed-loop lighting control has used measured daylight as its primary input for decades, and at least one granted patent (Apple US11419197) already learns from user manual adjustments with measured ambient light as context. Not novel.

### Inferences
- "No preferences UI; learn only from overrides" is the Nest product thesis (2011 filings) transplanted to lighting, and ABL IP Holding has already transplanted it to lighting with time-of-day context and an ML model.
- The "rainy day is a measured light signature, not a weather-API forecast" framing is a *marketing* differentiator, not a patentable one. Substituting a measured sensor value for a forecast value as an input feature is the archetypal obvious variation — and it is in any case the older, more conventional choice in lighting control, since the whole daylight-harvesting field measures rather than forecasts.
- Possible narrow residue: learning per-*outcome* (Read/Relax/Cook) target *room-state vectors* — i.e. the override updates the target of a closed-loop solver rather than a device setting. That coupling of items 1+2+3 is the founder's strongest (but still narrow, obviousness-exposed) position.

### Gaps
- Assignee, filing date and legal status of US9814117, US10778462, US11357090 unverified.
- I found no patent expressly reciting "measured outdoor illuminance as a feature of a learned-preference context vector" as a claim limitation — but given the blocked databases this is not a novelty finding.

## Key Question 4 — CONCENTRIC RING CONTACTS (rotationally symmetric mating) + battery pack held electrically dead until mated

### Takeaway
Both halves are old. Concentric annular contacts allowing relative rotation are documented in a **1980** US patent, and rotationally-symmetric battery packs designed to be rotated into engagement are in recent granted art. "Dead until mated" is universal safety practice in battery/charger and magnetic-connector design with extensive patent art (MagSafe-class and tool-battery-class). Not novel; at most a narrow combination claim on the specific geometry.

### Cited Findings

**Concentric / annular rotationally-symmetric contacts:**
- **US4209214 — "Movable electrical contact device"** (1980): "intended to provide contact between **two concentric members capable of undergoing relative rotation**, comprising at least one contact blade fixed to one member and **in rubbing contact with an annular conductive portion** fixed to the other member." This is the core concept, ~46 years old and long expired — [USPTO record](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/4209214) (page blocked; via search index)
- **CA2945326A1 — "Electrical connection system with annular contact"** — "a compressive force that holds a plug secure axially and rotationally with respect to a socket" — [ref](https://patents.google.com/patent/CA2945326A1/en)
- **US10916742 — "Battery pack, electrical device, and charging device"**: "The assembled battery is preferably disposed so as to be **rotationally symmetrical in a plan view**, meaning the shape agrees with a shape before rotation at every predetermined rotation angle. This allows the **rotation of the battery pack for rotatingly engaging the connector portion with the connection portion** to be performed more smoothly." Directly on point for "mates at any angle" — [USPTO record](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/10916742) (via search index)
- **US7619387 — "Battery pack attachment arrangement"** — rotational battery pack attachment

- "Dead until mated" is not merely prior art — it is effectively a **regulatory/safety requirement** for any exposed-contact battery interface (IEC 62368-1 / IEC 62133 class concerns). Claiming it would be claiming compliance.
- The concentric-ring geometry for angle-agnostic mating has been public since at least 1980, and applied specifically to battery packs in US10916742.
- Remaining residue: a *device claim* on the specific combination (two concentric annular contacts of stated diameters/widths on a swappable lighting battery pack, with a specific detection scheme that energises only after full annular engagement is sensed). That is a narrow, easily-designed-around claim — a competitor changes the contact count or the detection method.
- **A registered design (industrial design) is the more realistic protection for the connector's appearance; a utility patent here is weak.**

## BOTTOM LINE per claimed novelty

### Takeaway
Four of the five are already covered by identifiable prior art. One (item 5) is not a patent question at all. The only defensible filing strategy is a narrow combination claim linking items 1–3, plus registered designs for items 4–5 — and even the combination claim faces a serious obviousness challenge.

### Cited Findings (the specific references a founder would be filing over)
- **Item 1 — CALIBRATION SWEEP / LIGHT TRANSPORT MATRIX: ALREADY COVERED BY IDENTIFIABLE PRIOR ART. Do not file as drafted.** Blocking references: [Schoeneman et al., SIGGRAPH '93](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/166117.166135) (constrained least squares to a target); [Afshari et al., Energy & Buildings 68:242–253 (2014)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0378778813005331) (light transport matrix identified by least squares / pseudo-inverse); [US9439260B2, Philips/Signify, Pandharipande & Caicedo](https://patents.google.com/patent/US9439260B2/en) (discrete-mode sweep, sensor-to-target-location relationship); [US8457793B2 / US8909380B2, Enlighted](https://patents.google.com/patent/US8457793B2/en) (each device tested independently, measured at internal and surrounding sensors); closed-loop daylight harvesting generally ([Lutron white paper](https://assets.lutron.com/a/documents/daylighting_white-paper.pdf)).
- **Item 2 — TARGET-STATE CONTROL: ALREADY COVERED. This is what every scene system does, and the "solve for a measured room state" narrowing is also disclosed.** Blocking references: Philips "automatically rendering a lighting atmosphere" family (**US8463408**, **US9420673**) rendering device parameters from an abstract outcome description; [WO2017029368A1 / Signify](https://patents.google.com/patent/WO2017029368A1/fr) (scene = colour per spatial point, sensor feedback corrects shortfall); **US10485076** (select by illuminance contribution, solve to target illuminance); [US20060038959](https://patents.justia.com/patent/20060038959); [US10051707B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10051707B2/en); and the DALI/IEC 62386 scene machinery itself ([DALI Alliance](https://www.dali-alliance.org/standards/IEC62386.html)). "Including third-party luminaires" is the purpose of the standards, not an invention.
- **Item 3 — LEARNING FROM OVERRIDES ONLY: ALREADY COVERED; the measured-vs-forecast distinction does NOT differentiate.** Blocking references: [Nest US20130173064A1 / WO2013058820A1](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20130173064A1/en) (setpoint schedule modified from corrective manual input patterns); **US9814117** (inference engine correlating manual adjustments with time of day / location / task, writing rules to a preferences database); [ABL IP Holding US9795004 / US10009978](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10009978) (user adjustment + time of day + prior state fed to an ML algorithm); [Apple US11419197](https://patents.justia.com/patent/11419197) (learns from manual brightness adjustments with measured state as context). Measured daylight has been the input to closed-loop lighting control since the 1990s — substituting a sensor reading for an API forecast is a textbook obvious variation.
- **Item 4 — CONCENTRIC RING CONTACTS + DEAD UNTIL MATED: ALREADY COVERED. "Dead until mated" is universal safety practice with extensive art.** Blocking references: **US4209214** (1980) (annular conductive portion with relative rotation); **US10916742** (battery pack rotationally symmetrical in plan view, rotated into connector engagement); [CA2945326A1](https://patents.google.com/patent/CA2945326A1/en) (annular contact, axial+rotational retention); [US8193774B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US8193774) ("does not deliver power until it recognizes that it has been loaded"); [US7405536B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US7405536B2/en) (output gated on detected connection); [DE102005061532A1](https://patents.google.com/patent/DE102005061532A1/en) (de-energized connection); Apple MagSafe family [US7311526B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US7311526B2/en), **US10020608**, [US10547151B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10547151). **NOVEL ONLY IN A NARROW COMBINATION** of specific geometry + specific mating-detection scheme — and trivially designed around.
- **Item 5 — THREE-LAYER CIRCULAR MODULE: NOT A PATENT QUESTION. No utility novelty; pursue a registered design.** Existing art/lookalikes: [EP3292348A1](https://patents.google.com/patent/EP3292348A1/en); **US10859241**; [US7703951B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US7703951B2/en) (circular chassis + circular PCB); **US6641283** (puck light, annular sidewall, detachable base); [CA2700924C](https://patents.google.com/patent/CA2700924C/en) (magnetically attached luminaire with magnetic electrical connections); [US20090267540A1](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20090267540A1/en) (user-replaceable modules); US class [362/157 self-powered lamp](https://patents.justia.com/patents-by-us-classification/362/157). Commercial: Philips Hue Go, under-cabinet puck lights, Nanoleaf, 48V magnetic track disc heads.

### Inferences
- The strongest — and only — plausible utility filing is a **single combination claim spanning items 1+2+3**: commission a per-room source→sensor contribution model; map a semantic outcome label to a measured target room-state vector; solve constrained least squares with measured daylight as a fixed additive term; and update the *target vector for that outcome label* (not device settings) from manual overrides conditioned on measured ambient light. Every limb is disclosed; the value is in the specific coupling. Expect a novelty allowance to be possible but an inventive-step (India s.2(1)(ja) / EPO Art. 56) objection to be likely and hard.
- Given absolute worldwide novelty under Indian law (s.2(1)(l), s.13), the SIGGRAPH '93 paper and the 2013–2016 Philips Research papers are as fatal as any patent and cannot be worked around by filing only in India.
- Practical recommendation: budget for a **professional novelty/FTO search** on CPC H05B47/* (especially H05B47/105, /11, /115, /155, /165, /19) and G05B13/* before drafting, and reallocate protection for items 4 and 5 from patents to **registered designs** plus trade-secret/know-how on the calibration implementation.
