## (A1) Is a self-ballasted LED luminaire / LED light covered under the CRS Order, and which exact categories and Indian Standards apply?

### Takeaway
Yes — LED lighting is squarely inside the Compulsory Registration Scheme (Scheme II) under the *Electronics and Information Technology Goods (Requirements for Compulsory Registration) Order, 2021*, but coverage is **category-by-category by product form**, not by "it emits light". The categories that exist are fixed general-purpose (IS 10322 Pt 5 Sec 1), recessed (Sec 2), road/street (Sec 3), lighting chains (Sec 7) and emergency (Sec 8) luminaires; self-ballasted LED lamps (IS 16102); and LED module controlgear (IS 15885). The whole IS 10322 series was revised to a **2026 edition** with a concurrent-run deadline now extended to **2 February 2027**.

### Cited Findings
- LED luminaires were brought under CRS in **July 2013**, when BIS added several products under the scheme — [Corpseed: BIS Certification for LED Luminaires IS 10322 (Part 5)](https://www.corpseed.com/product/bis-certification-for-led-luminaires)
- The CRS lighting categories under IS 10322 Part 5 that carry their own registration line-items are:
  - **Part 5 / Section 1 — Fixed General Purpose LED Luminaires** — [Elitas: BIS Registration (CRS) for Fixed General Purpose LED Luminaires IS 10322 (Part 5/Sec 1)](https://www.elitasrcs.com/products/bis-registration-for-fixed-general-purpose-led-luminaries-10322-part-5-sec-1); [BL-India: IS 10322 (Part 5/Sec 1)](https://www.bl-india.com/products/fixed-general-purpose-led-luminaires); [BRL India](https://brlindia.com/bis-registration-for-fixed-general-purpose-led-luminaires-is-10322/)
  - **Part 5 / Section 2 — Recessed LED Luminaires**; BIS mandates certification "to mitigate risks such as electric shock, overheating, insulation failure, and fire hazards" — [EVTL India](https://evtlindia.com/bis-crs/bis-certification-for-recessed-led-luminaires-is-10322-part-5-section-2-2012); [Elitas](https://www.elitasrcs.com/products/bis-registration-for-recessed-led-luminaries-is-10322-part-5-section-2)
  - **Part 5 / Section 3 — LED Luminaires for Road and Street Lighting** — [Elitas](https://www.elitasrcs.com/products/bis-registration-for-led-luminaires-for-road-and-street-lighting-is-10322-part-5-section-3)
  - **Part 5 / Section 7 — LED Lighting Chains** (string/decorative lights) — [Elitas](https://www.elitasrcs.com/products/bis-registration-for-led-lighting-chains-is-10322-part-5-section-7)
  - **Part 5 / Section 8 — LED Luminaires for Emergency Lighting** — [EVTL India](https://evtlindia.com/bis-crs/bis-certification-for-led-luminaires-for-emergency-lighting-is-10322-part-5-section-8-2013); [Elitas](https://www.elitasrcs.com/products/bis-registration-for-led-luminaires-for-emergency-lighting-is-10322-part-5-section-8); [BRL India](https://brlindia.com/crs-registration-for-emergency-lighting-is-10322/)
- **IS 10322 (Part 5/Section 4)** is the Indian Standard for **"Portable general purpose luminaires"**, the BIS adoption of IEC 60598-2-4 — [IS 10322-5-4 on Internet Archive (BIS document)](https://archive.org/details/gov.in.is.10322.5.4.1987)
- **IS 15885 (Part 2/Sec 13)** — "DC or AC Supplied Electronic Control Gear for LED Modules" is a separate CRS line item with its own registration — [Elitas: BIS CRS Registration for DC or AC Supplied Electronic Control Gear for LED Modules, IS 15885 Part 2 Sec 13](https://www.elitasrcs.com/products/bis-registration-for-dc-or-ac-supplied-electronic-control-gear-for-led-modules-is-15885-part-2-sec-13)
- **IS 10322 (Part 1):2026** has been introduced under the CRO, replacing the 2014 version — [Aleph India: BIS Issues Guidelines for LED Luminaires IS 10322](https://alephindia.in/bis-issues-guidelines-for-led-luminaires-is-10322.php); [Sanyog Conformity](https://www.sanyogconformity.com/blog/bis-certification-for-led-lights-in-india-introduction/)
- BIS issued **official implementation guidelines for the revised IS 10322:2026 series on 30 March 2026** — [CSA Group global regulatory update](https://www.csagroup.org/global-certification-regulatory-update/india-bis-issues-implementation-guidelines-for-revised-is-103222026-series-for-led-luminaires/); [C-PRAV Group](https://c-prav.com/2026/03/30/indias-bis-issues-implementation-guidelines-for-revised-is-103222026-series-for-led-luminaires)
- The 2026 revision introduces **IPX9 ingress protection, electromagnetic field (EMF) safety, expanded photobiological safety, protection against fast-rotating parts, PELV/SELV provisions and updated shock-protection limits**, and luminaire registrations not updated to the 2026 versions "can be removed" — [SS Global Services: IS 10322 Series 2026 Implementation Guidelines](https://www.ssglobalservices.in/news-updates/bis-crs-led-luminaires-is-10322-series-2026-update)
- **The compliance deadline was extended from 2 August 2026 to 2 February 2027**; the concurrent-run period (old + new standard both acceptable) now runs to 02 Feb 2027 — [Elitas: BIS Extends LED Luminaires Standard Deadline to Feb 2027](https://www.elitasrcs.com/articles/bis-extends-led-luminaires-standard-deadline-to-feb-2027); [UMS PCS: BIS Revised Series Guidelines for Luminaires and Lighting Systems 2026](https://umspcs.in/news-updates/revised-series-guidelines-for-luminaires-and-lighting-systems-2026/)
- **MeitY circular dated 3 June 2026** introduced revised *series guidelines* alongside the revision of IS 16102 (Part 1):2026 and IS 10322:2026 — [UMS PCS](https://umspcs.in/news-updates/revised-series-guidelines-for-luminaires-and-lighting-systems-2026/)
- The primary BIS implementation document exists at `crsbis.in/BIS/app_srv/tdc/gl/docs/Guidelines_for_Implementation_of_Revised_Standards_for_LED_Luminaires.pdf` (surfaced in search; **could not be fetched — domain blocked by proxy**)

### Inferences
- **The Modulark "LED light head on a wall disc" does not map cleanly onto any existing CRS category.** It is not recessed, not road/street, not a lighting chain, and not emergency lighting. If it is mains-fed and permanently/semi-permanently mounted to a wall, BIS would most likely classify it as a **Fixed General Purpose LED Luminaire, IS 10322 (Part 5/Sec 1):2026**. That is the category to plan around for the mains variant.
- Because the product is *modular*, BIS may treat the wall disc (mains-connected, contains the driver) and the light head separately from the battery pack. If the wall disc contains AC→DC LED driver electronics that are sold as a distinct article, **IS 15885 (Part 2/Sec 13) controlgear registration could be triggered as a second, separate registration**. If the driver is integral and never sold separately, it is normally assessed as part of the luminaire.
- **IS 16102 (self-ballasted LED lamps)** is for lamps with a cap (B22/E27 etc.) that replace a bulb. It does **not** apply to this product form. Do not let a consultant push you into it.
- The IS 10322:2026 transition is a live trap for a company certifying now: certify against the **2026 edition directly**, not the 2012/2014 editions, or the registration is obsolete within months.

### Gaps
- The exact serial-number entries in the CRO 2021 Schedule for each lighting category could not be read (both `thc.nic.in` PDF and `bis.gov.in` Scheme II page blocked). The report should not state S. Nos. without verification.
- Whether **IS 10322 (Part 5/Sec 4) — portable general purpose luminaires** is itself a *notified CRS line item* is unresolved and is treated separately in (A2) below — this is the single most consequential open question for this product.

## (A2) Does a BATTERY-POWERED portable luminaire fall under CRS?

### Takeaway
**The evidence points to NO — and this may be the most valuable finding in the whole research set.** Multiple sources indicate BIS has clarified in its official CRS FAQ that **portable luminaires and luminaires under IS 10322 that run on cell/battery (with the sole exception of emergency luminaires) are exempted from the Order**, and that "LED portable lights are not covered under CRO". This is a genuine regulatory off-ramp for a battery-only product form — but it is a *product-classification* argument that must be confirmed in writing with BIS before it is relied on commercially.

### Cited Findings
- "BIS has clarified in official FAQ regarding Portable Luminaire and Luminaires under IS 10322 with battery (except Emergency Luminaire) that they are exempted from the order." — search synthesis of [Vincular: LED Lighting (CRS-BIS Regulatory updates)](https://www.vincular.in/led-lighting-crs-bis-regulatory-updates) (page itself blocked by proxy; claim surfaced in search result text)
- "**LED Portable lights are not covered under CRO**" — search synthesis of the BIS CRS FAQ documents hosted at crsbis.in, specifically [CRS FAQ Rev 13, Nov 2018](https://www.crsbis.in/BIS/app_srv/tdc/gl/docs/FAQ_16_11_2018_Rev_13.pdf), [Modified FAQs](https://www.crsbis.in/BIS/app_srv/tdc/gl/docs/Modified_FAQs.pdf), [Revised FAQs (Phase IV products)](https://www.crsbis.in/BIS/app_srv/tdc/gl/docs/Revised_FAQs.pdf) (documents located but **not readable — crsbis.in blocked by proxy**)
- The FAQ set explicitly contains a question on "whether luminaires with Cell/Battery [are] covered under the scope of IS" — confirming BIS has addressed the point formally, though the answer text could not be retrieved
- **Contradicting view:** one consultancy states "BIS CRS applies to both AC and DC LED luminaires, and **DC operation (battery or solar-based) does not change CRS applicability**" — [Instacertify: LED Light, Street Light & Solar Street Light Compliance](https://instacertify.com/blog/led-light-street-light-solar-street-light-compliance/)
- The IS 10322:2026 revision itself adds "updated provisions for battery-operated luminaires and marking, as well as PELV/SELV provisions" — [UMS PCS](https://umspcs.in/news-updates/revised-series-guidelines-for-luminaires-and-lighting-systems-2026/); [SS Global Services](https://www.ssglobalservices.in/news-updates/bis-crs-led-luminaires-is-10322-series-2026-update)

### Inferences
- The apparent contradiction is probably resolvable: the Instacertify claim is about **DC-input luminaires within a notified category** (e.g. a 24 V DC street light, which is still a Sec 3 road-lighting luminaire). The FAQ exemption is about **product form** — a *portable*, battery-carrying luminaire is not one of the notified Sections (1, 2, 3, 7, 8), so it falls outside the Order. Both statements can be true simultaneously. The report writer should present it this way, flagged as an inference.
- **Strategic consequence for Modulark:** the architecture (wall disc + swappable battery pack + light head) can potentially be positioned as a **battery-powered portable luminaire**, not a fixed mains luminaire — which would move the luminaire itself out of CRS entirely. The wall disc then becomes a *charger/dock*, not a luminaire.
- **But this creates a new CRS exposure, not zero exposure:** see (A3) — the lithium battery pack and the AC-fed charging dock each have their own compulsory registration paths. The exemption moves the burden, it does not delete it.
- The 2026 revision adding explicit battery-luminaire provisions suggests BIS is *tightening* around this space. The exemption should be treated as **potentially time-limited**, and re-checked before launch.

### Gaps
- **The FAQ exemption text has not been read first-hand.** This is the highest-value verification task in the entire project: pull `crsbis.in/BIS/app_srv/tdc/gl/docs/Revised_FAQs.pdf` and the latest FAQ revision, find the portable/battery-luminaire Q&A, and quote the exact wording.
- Whether the **2026 CRO amendments** (10 March 2026 / S.O. 1246(E), in force 15 June 2026, and the 3 Aug 2026 amendment referenced by one source) changed the portable/battery-luminaire position is **unverified**. Given that two amendments landed in 2026, the 2018-era FAQ answer may be stale.
- Whether a **mains-connected charging dock with a mains cord** ("wall disc") is itself a notified CRS article is unresolved — it could fall under adaptors/chargers or under nothing.

## (A3) Is the SENSING NODE separately covered? And what about the lithium battery pack?

### Takeaway
CRS is a **closed list of named product categories**, so a generic IoT sensor is only in scope if it matches a named entry — and the named entries have expanded fast (smart speakers, wearables, XR/AR/VR devices, CCTV cameras). More importantly, a finding the brief did not anticipate: **the swappable lithium battery pack is almost certainly a compulsory CRS article in its own right under IS 16046 (Part 2):2018**, and that is likely to be this product's single biggest unavoidable certification cost.

### Cited Findings
- **Lithium battery — the big one:** "Portable sealed secondary lithium cells and batteries are covered under **IS 16046 (Part 2):2018** in MeitY's Electronics and IT Goods (Requirement of Compulsory Registration) Order, 2021 — no manufacture, import or sale in India without a valid BIS CRS registration." Scope explicitly includes "18650 and 21700 cells, pouch and prismatic cells, and **packs built from them** for phones, laptops, power banks, wearables, toys, medical devices and portable equipment" — [PCN Global India: BIS CRS Registration for Lithium-Ion Cells & Batteries in India, IS 16046](https://pcnindiaglobal.com/2026/07/20/bis-crs-registration-lithium-ion-batteries-india/); [PCN Global (June 2026)](https://pcnindiaglobal.com/2026/06/10/bis-crs-registration-lithium-ion-batteries-india-is-16046/); [Elitas: IS 16046 Part 1](https://www.elitasrcs.com/products/bis-registration-for-sealed-secondary-cells-batteries-containing-alkaline-or-other-non-acid-electrolytes-for-use-in-portable-applications-is-16046)
- IS 16046 Parts 1 and 2 align closely with **IEC 62133-1 and IEC 62133-2** — [Global Approbation: Lithium-Ion Battery Certification India](https://www.global-approbation.com/blogs/lithium-ion-battery-certification-india.html)
- **New (2026):** by MeitY order dated **3 February 2026**, rated-capacity verification per Clause 7.3.1 of IS 16047 (Part 3):2018 is now mandatory for lithium batteries; existing licensees must comply by **30 April 2027** — [PSR Compliance](https://www.psrcompliance.com/blog/bis-certification-lithium-ion-batteries-ev-packs-is-16046); BIS guidance document at [crsbis.in guidelines_for_rated_capacity.pdf](https://www.crsbis.in/BIS/app_srv/tdc/gl/docs/guidelines_for_rated_capacity.pdf) (located, not fetchable)
- **CRS scope expansion 2024–2026:** BIS/MeitY added "wireless earbuds, smartwatches, **IoT sensors**, and EV chargers" per a MeitY notification stated to be from **October 2024**; smart speakers (with and without display) were also included — [Instacertify: BIS CRS Registration Guide](https://instacertify.com/blog/bis-crs-registration/); [Sunren: IoT Device Certification India](https://www.sunren.net/blog/iot-device-certification-india/)
- **Extended Reality (XR/AR/VR/MR) devices** brought under compulsory registration via a MeitY gazette notification of **7 November 2025** — [Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/digital/meity-updates-safety-standards-for-electronic-goods-adds-er-ar-vr-devices-under-compulsory-certification-83858.htm); [TÜV Rheinland: India – BIS CRS Scheme (Product Addition)](https://www.tuv.com/regulations-and-standards/en/india-bis-crs-scheme-product-addition.html)
- **Gazette Notification S.O. 1246(E) dated 10 March 2026** substantively amended the CRO 2021; **in force from 15 June 2026** — [India Law: MeitY's 2026 Amendment to the Compulsory Registration Framework for Electronics Goods](https://www.indialaw.in/blog/cro-rules-2026-specialized-equipment/)
- **CCTV cameras** added as **S. No. 41** of the CRO Schedule — [Absolute Veritas: BIS Amendment to the Electronics and IT Goods Order](https://absoluteveritas.com/bis-amendment-to-the-electronics-and-information-technology-goods-/)
- A further **amendment dated 3 August 2026** reportedly added Television Sets and mandated display of the Standard Mark for specified goods — [Absolute Veritas](https://absoluteveritas.com/bis-amendment-to-the-electronics-and-information-technology-goods-/) *(single source, low confidence)*
- "A large number of IoT devices are required to be registered under the Compulsory Registration Scheme (CRS)" — [Karparivartan: BIS CRS Registration Complete Guide](https://www.karparivartan.com/bis-crs-registration-in-india-complete-guide-to-compulsory-registration-scheme/)

### Inferences
- **The lithium pack is the hard, unavoidable cost.** A *swappable* pack is a separately-marketed article, which removes any "integral battery, assessed with the host" argument. Budget for a full IS 16046 (Part 2) registration plus IEC 62133-2-style safety testing (crush, impact, overcharge, forced discharge, thermal abuse, short circuit). This testing is destructive, sample-hungry and slower than luminaire testing. **If the battery cells are imported, confirm the cell vendor already holds an Indian CRS registration for the cell — it does not automatically cover your pack, but an uncertified cell makes the pack registration far harder.**
- The claim that "IoT sensors" were added to CRS in an October 2024 MeitY notification appears in consultancy marketing copy and I could **not corroborate it against a gazette citation**. Treat as **low confidence**. The corroborated additions are smart speakers, wearables/smartwatches, wireless earbuds, XR devices, CCTV cameras, TVs.
- **Practical read for the sensing node:** a battery-powered room sensor with a BLE/Wi-Fi radio, that is not a speaker, not a camera, not a wearable, not a gateway and not an XR device, most likely falls **outside** the named CRS categories today. But (i) if it carries a rechargeable lithium cell, IS 16046 bites; (ii) if it ships with a **mains USB power adaptor**, that adaptor is a CRS article (adaptors/chargers are long-standing CRS items) — buy a pre-registered adaptor from a vendor who will give you their R-number rather than certifying your own; (iii) CRS scope is expanding roughly annually, so this should be re-checked at launch.
- Because CRS is enumerated rather than principle-based, **the winning move is classification work, not testing work**. Getting the product described so it lands outside the enumerated categories (or inside a cheap one) is worth more than any test-lab optimisation.

### Gaps
- No primary-source confirmation that "IoT sensors" are a named CRO Schedule entry. The CRO 2021 Schedule as amended to date must be read directly.
- Could not confirm whether the 10 March 2026 / 15 June 2026 amendment touched lighting or IoT sensing categories.

## (A4) Is there a separate BIS ISI mark (Scheme I) requirement distinct from CRS (Scheme II)?

### Takeaway
Yes — the two schemes are genuinely distinct legal instruments and both exist in 2026, but on current evidence **lighting sits in CRS (Scheme II / CRO), not in the new appliances QCO (Scheme I / ISI)**. A brand-new *Safety of Household, Commercial and Similar Electrical Appliances (Quality Control) Order, 2026* comes into force **1 October 2026** and makes ISI marking under Scheme I mandatory for the products it lists — whether a luminaire or a charging dock is caught by that list could not be verified and is an urgent open question given the date.

### Cited Findings
- CRS is formally **"Scheme – II (Registration Scheme)"** under BIS product certification — [BIS: Scheme II (Registration Scheme)](https://www.bis.gov.in/product-certification/products-under-compulsory-certification/scheme-ii-registration-scheme/?lang=en) *(page blocked by proxy; title and framing confirmed via search)*
- "The new order will be called **Safety of Household, Commercial and Similar Electrical Appliances (Quality Control) Order, 2026**. It shall come into force on the **1st of October, 2026**. BIS certification (**ISI Mark**) is made mandatory under **Scheme-I** for the products that have been specified in this QCO." — [BL-India: BIS QCO for Electrical Appliances Explained – 2026](https://www.bl-india.com/notifications/isi-bis-certification/bis-qco-for-electrical-appliances-2026); [CliniExperts regulatory update](https://cliniexperts.com/regulatory-update/safety-of-household-commercial-and-similar-electrical-appliances-quality-control-order-2026/); [SS Global Services](https://www.ssglobalservices.in/news-updates/bis-certification-electrical-appliances-qco-2026-india)
- The appliances QCO 2026 is built on **IS 302 (Part 1):2024** (the Indian adoption of IEC 60335-1, safety of household appliances) — [BIS Certifications: QCO 2026 – IS 302 (Part 1):2024](https://bis-certifications.com/ministry-updates/bis-qco-2026-is-302-part-1-2024-electrical-appliances); [Standphill India: QCO 2026](https://www.standphillindia.in/household-commercial-electrical-appliances-qco-2026.php)
- LED luminaire standard revisions "are covered by the **Compulsory Registration Order (CRO)**", i.e. handled in the CRS/Scheme II framework rather than the appliances QCO — [BIS Certification for LED Lights in India](https://www.sanyogconformity.com/blog/bis-certification-for-led-lights-in-india-introduction/)
- A "Transition Facilitation QCO 2026" also exists, easing transitions for manufacturers/importers/foreign suppliers — [UMS PCS: Transition Facilitation QCO 2026](https://umspcs.in/blog/transition-facilitation-qco-quality-control-order-2026/)

### Inferences
- The clean distinction to give the founder: **Scheme I (ISI licence)** is a *factory* licence — BIS audits your manufacturing premises, your in-house testing capability and your quality control, and you mark with the ISI monogram. **Scheme II (CRS registration)** is a *product model* registration based on a test report from a BIS-recognised lab plus a self-declaration — **no factory audit**. For a garage-scale hardware startup, CRS is survivable; a Scheme I ISI licence, with its factory-inspection and in-house-lab expectations, is a far heavier lift and is where 3D-printed, hand-assembled production would attract the most scrutiny.
- Luminaires appear to be a CRS-only story today. **But the 1 October 2026 appliances QCO is nine days away from the date of this research** and the product scope was not verifiable — this needs checking immediately, not eventually.

### Gaps
- The product list annexed to the Safety of Household, Commercial and Similar Electrical Appliances (QCO), 2026 could not be read. Does it capture battery chargers, docking stations, or luminaires? Unknown.
- Whether any Scheme I ISI requirement attaches to the lithium pack (as opposed to the CRS route) was not checked.

## (A5) CRS process, costs, timelines, and the model/variant question

### Takeaway
CRS is a **registration**, not a licence: test at a BIS-recognised lab, file on the BIS CRS portal, get an R-number, self-declare conformity on the label. Realistic all-in cost is roughly **₹30,000–₹50,000 per registered model** plus consultant fees; realistic elapsed time is **6–9 weeks**, not the 20–25 working days quoted nominally. Registration is valid **2 years**, renewal ₹25,000. Crucially, **"series guidelines" let one registration cover multiple models (typically up to 10)** — this is the mechanism that makes low-volume, multi-variant products economically survivable.

### Cited Findings
**Fees**
- "The inclusion fee is **₹15,000 (includes one test)**, with each **additional test report costing ₹10,000**." — [Green Permits: CRS BIS Registration Fees, Timeline and Document Checklist](https://www.greenpermits.in/10/crs-bis-registration-fees-timeline-and-document-checklist/)
- "Testing costs per model generally range from **₹10,000 to ₹30,000**, depending on complexity and test parameters." — [Green Permits](https://www.greenpermits.in/10/crs-bis-registration-fees-timeline-and-document-checklist/)
- "A single product with one test report may cost around **₹30,000–₹35,000 overall**, while complex models or foreign applications may reach **₹50,000 or more** including ancillary costs." — [JS Certification: BIS Certification Cost in India 2026](https://jscertification.in/bis-certification-cost-in-india-2026-fees-process/)
- "BIS CRS registration remains valid for **two years**, and the **renewal fee is ₹25,000 per application**." — [Green Permits](https://www.greenpermits.in/10/crs-bis-registration-fees-timeline-and-document-checklist/); [SEYECS: BIS CRS Certification India 2026 Guide](https://seyecs.com/services/product-compliance/bis-crs/)

### Inferences
- **Realistic budget for the founder, per registered article:** ₹15,000 BIS inclusion fee + ₹10,000–30,000 lab testing + consultant ₹25,000–75,000 (typical Indian compliance-consultancy engagement; **note: I found no citable rupee figure for consultant fees — this range is my estimate and must be marked as such**) ≈ **₹50,000–₹1,20,000 per article, all-in, first time**. With a mains luminaire + a lithium pack + possibly a controlgear entry, a worst-case CRS bill is **₹1.5L–₹3L** before a single unit is sold. Renewals ₹25,000 per article every two years.
- **The series mechanism is the cost lever.** Design the product family so that wattage/colour-temperature/finish variants are *models within one series* (same platform, same driver topology, same enclosure family, same safety-relevant construction), tested on the worst case. Ten models under one ₹15,000 registration is a completely different economics from ten registrations. Changing the enclosure material, the driver, or the protection class is what breaks a series and forces a new registration.
- The nominal 20–25 working days assumes the lab has capacity and your documents are perfect. For a first-time applicant with a novel product form, **plan 3 months** from "design frozen" to "R-number in hand", and remember the design must be frozen — you cannot iterate the enclosure after sample submission.
- An **Indian manufacturer registers directly**; the AIR (Authorised Indian Representative) apparatus exists only for foreign manufacturers. Being an Indian Pvt Ltd assembling in India is a real simplification here. **Note:** I could not verify whether PCBAs imported and assembled in India makes you a "manufacturer" or an "importer" in BIS's eyes — this affects documentation and is worth a direct query to BIS.

### Gaps
- **No citable consultant fee figures.** Do not print a rupee range for consultants without sourcing it.
- The exact text of the 3 June 2026 series guidelines (what construction differences break a series) could not be obtained — this is directly load-bearing for the multi-variant cost question and should be pulled from crsbis.in.

## (B) CAN A 3D-PRINTED ENCLOSURE PASS? — the central risk question

### Takeaway
**Direct verdict: for a mains-connected luminaire, no — in-house FDM printing is not a viable route to a BIS-certified enclosure, and injection moulding (or a pre-certified off-the-shelf enclosure) is effectively mandatory. For a battery-only SELV product, the answer flips to a qualified yes.** Three independent problems kill the mains case: (1) the flammability tests are severe and material-specific, (2) a filament's UL94 V-0 rating is earned on injection-moulded plaques and **does not transfer to a printed part** — UL has a separate "Blue Card" precisely for this, and (3) FDM's unit-to-unit variability defeats the standards' implicit assumption that every production unit is identical to the tested sample. The SELV escape route is real and is the key strategic insight.

### Cited Findings — what the tests actually demand
- **Glow wire:** "Parts are subjected to a test using a nickel-chromium glow-wire heated to **650°C**. Any flame or glowing of the sample shall extinguish **within 30 seconds** of withdrawing the glow-wire, and any burning or molten drop shall **not ignite a single layer of tissue paper** spread out horizontally 200 mm ± 5 mm below the sample." — [TRILUX: Glow-Wire Resistance According to EN 60598](https://www.trilux.com/en/lighting-practice/lighting-equipment/luminaires/fire-protection/resistance-to-glow-wire/); [D.L.S. Electronic Systems: IEC/EN 60598 Luminaires Part 1](https://www.dlsemc.com/iec-en-60598-product-safety-luminaires-part-1-general-requirements-and-tests/)
- **The severity escalates for parts holding live parts:** "IEC 60598-1 requires that **insulating parts supporting live parts** of a certain wattage must pass the **960°C** glow wire test." — [LISUN: Requirements and Judgment Standards for Glow Wire Testing](https://www.lisungroup.com/news/technology-news/requirements-and-judgment-standards-for-glow-wire-testing.html); [Glow Wire Test at 960°C: Key Standards](https://www.ledphotometer.com/blogs/glow-wire-test-at-960c-standards/)
- **Conversely, the exemption exists in the standard:** "for insulation materials which **do not fixate live (active) components** in their position, glow wire testing is stipulated by EN 60598" at the lower severity — [TRILUX](https://www.trilux.com/en/lighting-practice/lighting-equipment/luminaires/fire-protection/resistance-to-glow-wire/)
- **The full heat/fire battery:** "To verify compliance with heat resistance requirements, **ball pressure test, glow wire test, CTI test and needle flame test** are performed at the values specified in the standard. The outer sections of the non-metallic material, the sections made of insulating material that support the energetic sections, including connections, must be heat resistant." — [LVT: IEC 60598-1 / TS EN 60598-1](https://www.lvt.com.tr/en/makale/ts-en-60598-1-aydinlatma-armaturleri)
- **IP rating is an enclosure requirement:** "The enclosure of a luminaire shall provide the degree of protection against the ingress of dust, solid objects and moisture in accordance with the classification of the luminaire and the IP number marked on the luminaire." — [LISUN: IEC 60598 Ingress Protection of Luminaires](https://www.lisungroup.com/news/technology-news/iec-60598-ingress-protection-of-luminaires.html)
- Current edition context: [IEC 60598-1 Edition 9.0 (2020-08)](https://www.lisungroup.com/standards/iec-60598-1-edition-9-luminaires-part-1-general-requirements-and-tests.html); [IEC 60598-1:2024 / EN IEC 60598-1:2024](https://webstore.iec.ch/en/publication/66620); IS 10322 Part 1:2026 is India's adoption
- The IS 10322:2026 revision **raises the enclosure bar further** by adding IPX9 provisions — [SS Global Services](https://www.ssglobalservices.in/news-updates/bis-crs-led-luminaires-is-10322-series-2026-update)

### Cited Findings — whether printed parts can carry a V-0 rating
- **This is the decisive finding.** "UL recognizes plastics under different programs depending on how the material has been tested. A **Blue Card** indicates that the material has been tested and certified by UL **for additive manufacturing** with specific parameters. A **Yellow Card** indicates that the material has been tested using **conventional manufacturing methods, but not specifically through the additive manufacturing process**. Both cards provide useful information on flammability performance; however, a Blue Card provides stronger support for 3D printed applications because the material has been tested in the relevant manufacturing process." — [Forge Labs: UL-94 Fire Safety Standards in Additive Manufacturing](https://forgelabs.com/blog/ul-94-fire-safety-standards-additive-manufacturing)
- "**UL-94 ratings are thickness-specific**, with a material rated V-0 at 3 mm potentially only achieving V-1 at 1 mm, and **3D printing parameters can influence flame retardant properties**." — [Forge Labs](https://forgelabs.com/blog/ul-94-fire-safety-standards-additive-manufacturing)
- Material vendors explicitly disclaim: "3D products have **not been designed, manufactured or tested by HP for compliance with legal requirements** for specific 3D printed parts and their uses; recipients are responsible for determining the suitability of 3D printed products for their uses" — [HP 3D High Reusability PA 12 UL94 / UL 746A certification document](https://forerunner3d.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/HP-PA12-glass-bead-UL94-and-UL-746A-certification.pdf); [HP PA 12 UL94 and UL 746A certification (Sculpteo)](https://www.sculpteo.com/media/imagecontent/UL%2094%20and%20UL%20746A%20Certification.pdf)
- "**Material properties may vary based on part geometry, print orientation, print settings, and temperature**" — vendor statement on FR filament, confirming anisotropy is acknowledged by the suppliers themselves — [SUNLU ABS-FR product knowledge](https://uk.store.sunlu.com/blogs/product-knowledge/sunlu-abs-fr-filament-ul94-v-0-flame-retardant-glass-fiber-abs-for-high-safety-engineering-applications)

### Cited Findings — what FR filaments exist
- **UL94 V-0 rated FDM filaments are commercially available.** Examples: ABS-FR V0 (1.75 mm, ±0.03 mm tolerance, standard FDM) marketed explicitly for "prototyping **electrical enclosures**" and "end-use production" — [Inslogic 3D: ABS FR V0 Filament, UL94 V-0 Certified](https://store.inslogic3d.com/products/abs-fr-v0-filament); [SUNLU ABS-FR (halogen-free FR additives, glass-fibre)](https://uk.store.sunlu.com/blogs/product-knowledge/sunlu-abs-fr-filament-ul94-v-0-flame-retardant-glass-fiber-abs-for-high-safety-engineering-applications); [3DTrcek ABS V0 UL94 FR filament](https://3dtrcek.com/en/abs-flame-retardant-v0-filament)
- Broader FR-material landscape in AM (including PC-FR, PEI/ULTEM, FR nylons): [Xometry: Flame Retardant 3D Printing Plastics](https://www.xometry.com/3d-printed-flame-retardant-plastics/); [3Dnatives: Flame-Retardant Materials for 3D Printing](https://www.3dnatives.com/en/the-top-flame-retardant-materials-for-3d-printing-050420244/); [Newmatik: Flame Retardant UL V0 3D Print Filaments](https://www.newmatik.com/knowledge-base/flame-retardant-ul-v0-3d-print-filaments); [Jellypipe: Flame-retardant 3D printing materials to UL-94 V0](https://www.jellypipe.com/en/blog-news/flame-retardant-3d-printing-materials-1/); [Nanovia: Fire resistant 3D printer filaments — norms and tests](https://nanovia.tech/en/fire-resistant-3d-printer-filaments-norms-and-tests/); [UltiMaker: Flame-retardant materials beginner's guide](https://ultimaker.com/learn/flame-retardant-materials-a-beginners-guide/)
- Practical constraint on printing them: "An **enclosed printing chamber is a must** and an air filtration system is recommended when using flame-retardant filaments." — [SUNLU](https://uk.store.sunlu.com/blogs/product-knowledge/sunlu-abs-fr-filament-ul94-v-0-flame-retardant-glass-fiber-abs-for-high-safety-engineering-applications)

- Context on UL 94 vs UL 746C (the end-product enclosure standard, which adds weathering, impact and long-term thermal ageing beyond the bench flame test): [Polycase: UL 94 vs UL 746C](https://www.polycase.com/techtalk/learningcenter/ul-94-vs-ul-746c)

### Inferences — the honest material-by-material verdict
*(The ball-pressure temperatures and polymer glass-transition figures below are standard engineering knowledge, not drawn from a fetched source in this pass — flag them as needing a datasheet citation before publication.)*

- **PLA — dead on arrival.** Glass transition around 55–60°C. The ball pressure test for external enclosure parts is typically 75°C, and 125°C for parts retaining live parts. PLA will deform under the 5 mm ball well below 75°C. It also drips when burned. PLA is disqualified for anything in a certified luminaire, mains or battery. **Not even a maybe.**
- **PETG** — Tg around 80°C, marginally above the 75°C external-parts threshold, with no margin, and it is not flame-retardant. It will not pass 750°C/960°C glow wire. Unsuitable for live-part retention; conceivably acceptable as a purely decorative, non-structural, non-live-part-retaining trim piece on a SELV product.
- **ABS / ASA** — Tg around 100–105°C, so ball pressure at 75°C is comfortable and 125°C is borderline. Standard (non-FR) ABS is **HB rated and burns readily** — it will fail glow wire and needle flame. **FR-ABS (V-0) is the only serious FDM candidate.**
- **PC and PC-FR** — best thermal performance of the accessible FDM materials (Tg ~145°C), clears both ball-pressure thresholds, and PC-FR grades reach V-0. Hard to print well: warping, moisture sensitivity, needs a high-temperature hotend and heated enclosure. This is the material a serious attempt would use.
- **Nylon / PA** — hygroscopic, dimensionally unstable with humidity, and that instability alone is disqualifying for a part that must hold an IP rating and a creepage/clearance geometry reproducibly.

### Inferences — the three structural problems
1. **The V-0 rating does not follow the polymer into your printer.** The Blue Card / Yellow Card distinction is the cleanest available proof of this: UL created a separate certification programme specifically because AM-processed parts behave differently. A filament spool advertising "UL94 V-0 certified" is, in almost every case, quoting a Yellow Card earned on a moulded 3 mm plaque. A BIS lab testing *your printed part* is testing a different object. Your printed part can genuinely fail a test the pellet passed.
2. **Anisotropy and porosity attack three different requirements at once.** Layer lines are preferential flame-propagation and melt-drip paths (glow wire, needle flame); inter-layer voids are leak paths that defeat any meaningful IP rating without post-processing (coating, gasketing, or vapour smoothing); and weak Z-axis layer adhesion means the impact/IK test result depends on print orientation. The standards' IP requirement is unconditional — the enclosure "shall provide the degree of protection ... in accordance with the classification" — with no allowance for process variation.
3. **The standards assume production units are identical to the tested sample; FDM does not deliver that.** CRS registration is granted against a test report on a submitted sample. An FDM part's properties shift with spool batch, filament moisture, nozzle wear, ambient temperature and slicer settings. There is no cited evidence that **BIS or IEC standards contemplate additive manufacturing at all** — I found none, and the absence matters: there is no AM-specific clause, no AM process-control annex, and no established route for a lab to certify "this printer, these parameters". You would be asking an assessor to accept an undocumented process. Some will simply refuse.

### Inferences — THE SELV / BATTERY-ONLY ESCAPE ROUTE (the strategic insight)
This is where the risk profile genuinely inverts, and the brief is right to flag it as potentially decisive.

- The flammability regime in IEC/IS 60598-1 is **graduated by what the part does, not by what the part is**. The cited TRILUX and LISUN material establishes the gradient explicitly: parts that *fixate live components* face the severe (960°C) glow-wire requirement; parts that *do not* face the lower stipulation. The standard's own logic is that **the hazard being guarded against is a fault current in a mains circuit igniting the plastic around it.**
- Remove the mains, and you remove the ignition source that the test exists to simulate. A **SELV product (nominally <60 V DC, and in this case more like 3.7–25 V)** running off a lithium pack has no mains-derived fault energy inside the enclosure. There is no live part in the regulatory sense for the enclosure to retain. The enclosure stops being a safety-critical fire barrier and becomes essentially a mechanical housing.
- Layer this on top of the (A2) finding — that **portable/battery luminaires under IS 10322 appear to be exempt from the CRO altogether** — and the battery-only variant potentially exits *both* the CRS registration requirement *and* the severe enclosure flammability regime in one move.
- **The residual hazard migrates to the battery**, which is exactly where Indian regulation puts it: IS 16046 (Part 2) / IEC 62133-2. A lithium pack can absolutely start a fire. So the battery-only architecture does not remove risk, it **relocates the compliance burden from an enclosure you print to a battery pack you buy** — and a battery pack is something you can source pre-certified from a vendor who already holds registration, whereas a certified 3D-printed enclosure is something nobody sells you.
- **The clean architecture this implies:**
  - **Wall disc = mains-connected charger/dock.** This is the only mains-touching article. Put the mains entry, the AC-DC conversion and all hazardous-voltage parts inside a **bought-in, already-certified enclosed power module or a certified off-the-shelf enclosure**, or simply use an **external, already-CRS-registered wall adaptor** and make the disc itself a low-voltage-only device. If the disc never sees mains voltage internally, its shell can be printed.
  - **Light head + battery pack = SELV, portable.** Printed shells are defensible here.
  - **Sensing node = SELV, battery.** Printed shell defensible.
- **The single highest-leverage design decision available to this company is to keep mains voltage out of every enclosure it prints.** That is worth more than any material substitution.

### Practical verdict (as demanded: direct, no hedging)
- **Mains-connected luminaire with an in-house FDM-printed enclosure: not viable for a BIS-certified, legally sellable product.** Do not plan the business around it. You would need PC-FR or FR-ABS, a Blue-Card-equivalent basis for the printed material, an IP strategy that survives layer porosity, and an assessor willing to accept an uncontrolled process — and you would still be re-qualifying every time you change a spool. The cost and schedule risk exceeds the cost of a mould.
- **Battery-only / SELV product with a printed enclosure: viable**, subject to confirming the portable-luminaire CRO exemption in writing and to certifying the battery pack properly. Print in FR-ABS or ASA anyway (not PLA, not PETG) — it costs little extra, it is defensible if anyone ever asks, and it protects you against a future rule change.
- **The industry-standard answer for small hardware companies is the hybrid, and it is the right one here:** 3D-print for prototypes, the pilot batch, and customer-discovery units; commit to **injection moulding at the point you need a certified mains product or volume above roughly 1,000–2,000 units**, where tooling amortises. Indian soft tooling / aluminium tooling for a small part is meaningfully cheaper than Western tooling. **I found no citable rupee figure for Indian tooling cost in this pass — flag as a gap, do not invent one.**
- **The third option the brief raises — a certified off-the-shelf enclosure — is underrated and should be evaluated seriously.** Buying a standard ABS/PC enclosure from a vendor that already holds the relevant flammability ratings converts your hardest compliance problem into a purchase order, at the cost of industrial-design freedom. For the *wall disc specifically* (the mains part, and the least design-critical of the three modules) this is very likely the correct answer.

### Gaps
- **No fetched source for polymer Tg values or for ball-pressure test temperatures (75°C / 125°C).** These are stated from engineering knowledge. Get filament datasheets and the IS 10322 Part 1:2026 / IEC 60695-10-2 text before publishing numbers.
- **I found no evidence either way on whether any BIS-recognised Indian lab has ever certified an additively-manufactured enclosure, or whether BIS has any published position on AM.** The absence of any AM provision in the standards is itself the finding, but a direct query to a BIS-recognised lab (e.g. via a consultant) would convert this from inference to fact and is cheap to do.
- No source found quantifying FDM unit-to-unit variation against the specific pass/fail margins of the glow-wire or ball-pressure tests.
- Indian injection-mould tooling costs: not researched, no figures.

## (C2) Does using a pre-certified module shortcut ETA? And is an import licence needed?

### Takeaway
**Yes, decisively.** The stated rule is that where RF modules are fitted **without any modification or alteration** into finished products **manufactured in India**, **no separate ETA is required for the finished product**. Additionally, an existing ETA-SD "may subsequently be utilized by other persons" — meaning a module vendor's ETA number is reusable downstream in a way that has **no clean FCC analogue**. Imports of delicensed-band equipment are exempt from the WPC import licence.

### Cited Findings
- "In cases where **RF modules are fitted without any modification or alteration in finished products manufactured in India, no separate ETA is required for such finished products**." — search synthesis across WPC/ETA guidance; see [Granite River Labs: WPC and ETA Certification](https://www.graniteriverlabs.com/en-us/market-access-services/country/india/wpc-mark) and [ASC Group: Equipment Type Approval (ETA) Licensing from WPC](https://www.ascgroup.in/service/equipment-type-approval-eta-licensing-from-wpc-department/)
- "Once ETA through self-declaration is generated for a model, it **may subsequently be utilized by other persons**." — [ASC Group](https://www.ascgroup.in/service/equipment-type-approval-eta-licensing-from-wpc-department/); [Quick License: WPC Certificate / ETA Certificate](https://www.quicklicense.in/wpc)
- **Worked example — Espressif holds Indian ETAs on its modules.** Espressif publishes the actual ETA certificate for a module: **ETA-SD-20200704087, ESP32-PICO-V3-ZERO** — [Espressif ETA-SD certificate (PDF)](https://documentation.espressif.com/ETA-SD-20200704087%20ESP32-PICO-V3-ZERO%20WPC.pdf)

- **Import licence:** ETA-SD covers equipment "exempted from import license requirements" in the delicensed bands — [DoT eServices: WPC – ETA (Self Declaration)](https://eservices.dot.gov.in/saral/wpc-eta-self-declaration). Import-licence mechanics and 2026 position: [IMARC Engineering](https://www.imarcengineering.com/blog/how-to-get-a-wpc-import-licence-in-india-for-wireless-equipment)
- Further ETA process/product guidance: [Karparivartan: WPC ETA Certification in India](https://www.karparivartan.com/wpc-eta-certification-in-india-complete-guide-to-process-products/); [BL-India: WPC ETA Certificate in India, Complete Guide 2026](https://www.bl-india.com/blogs/general/wpc-eta-certificate-in-india); [Elitas: WPC Certification / ETA Approval](https://www.elitasrcs.com/services/wpc-certification-eta-approval); [Standphill India: WPC ETA Certification](https://www.standphillindia.in/wpc-eta-certification.php); [Beacon Filing](https://beaconfiling.com/blog/wpc-eta-approval-wireless-equipment)

### Inferences
- **Comparison with FCC modular approval, as the brief asks.** FCC grants a module a *modular approval* that the integrator may rely on, but the integrator remains responsible for the host: antenna must match the grant, RF exposure must be re-evaluated, the host must carry the module's FCC ID via a "Contains FCC ID: …" label, and any deviation from the grant conditions (different antenna, different placement) forces a Class II permissive change or a fresh filing. **India's position as stated is looser**: "no separate ETA is required" for unmodified module integration in domestically-manufactured products, with no equivalent published permissive-change machinery. The practical industry answer in India is that integrators lean on the module's ETA and keep the module's certificate and test report on file.
- **However — and this is the caution the report should carry — the "without any modification or alteration" condition is doing all the work.** Changing the antenna (a very common integration change: chip antenna → PCB trace → external whip) is plausibly an "alteration" of the RF characteristics. If Modulark uses the module's own on-board antenna exactly as supplied, the exemption is solid. If it feeds an external or custom antenna, **file your own ETA-SD anyway** — at ₹10,000 and a few days, buying certainty is obviously correct and the argument is not worth having with a customs officer or a DoT inspector.
- **Concrete recommendation:** use a module that already holds an Indian ETA (Espressif publishes theirs; Nordic and Murata modules are widely used in India and likely hold them — **not verified in this pass**). Get the ETA certificate PDF and the module's EN 300 328 test report from the vendor *before* designing it in, and keep both in the technical file. Budget ₹10,000 + one week for your own ETA-SD as cheap insurance.
- Note that the **PCBAs are imported** in this product. The module arrives already mounted on an imported PCBA, not as a loose module fitted in India. Whether that counts as "finished products manufactured in India" (assembly in India, yes) or as importing an assembly containing an RF module (which triggers the "upload test reports of all RF modules" path) is **genuinely ambiguous** and worth clarifying with a consultant. The conservative and cheap answer is again: file your own ETA-SD.

### Gaps

- The precise official wording and source (WPC circular number) of the "no separate ETA for unmodified module integration" rule was not located — it is repeated across consultancies but I did not reach the primary WPC document. **This should be verified before it is relied on**, since it is the load-bearing claim of this section.

## (C3) TEC / MTCTE — does it apply, and how is it different from WPC ETA?

### Takeaway
**MTCTE almost certainly does not apply to this product, and it is a genuinely different regime from ETA.** MTCTE is now in **Phase VI, mandatory from 24 August 2025**, covering 14 categories of *telecom network equipment* — IoT **gateways**, cellular CPE, fixed wireless telephones, LAN switches, IP routers. A Wi-Fi/BLE-equipped luminaire and a BLE sensor node are **endpoints, not gateways**, and do not appear in the phase lists.

### Cited Findings
- MTCTE was introduced through the **Indian Telegraph (Amendment) Rules, 2017**, making it mandatory for telecom equipment to be tested and certified before sale, import or use in India — [UL Solutions: Mandatory Testing and Certification for Telecommunication Equipment in India](https://www.ul.com/insights/mandatory-testing-and-certification-telecommunication-equipment-india)
- **Phase VI:** product list announced **25 February 2025**; **mandatory from 24 August 2025** — "From 24 August 2025, it is prohibited to import, sell, distribute, or use the equipment unless certified under MTCTE requirements" — [SGS Taiwan: India TEC Announces Mandatory Certification Date for Phase VI Products](https://www.sgs.com.tw/en/news-media-resources-content/page?id=1630); [TÜV SÜD: India — Products under phase VI of MTCTE published](https://www.tuvsud.com/en/knowledge-hub/technical-updates/consumer-products-and-retail-essentials/india-products-under-phase-vi-of-mtcte-published); [cetecom advanced](https://cetecomadvanced.com/en/news/india-implements-new-telecommunication-equipment-standards-under-phase-vi-of-mtcte/)
- "A total of **14 categories** of products are included in MTCTE supervision, including **cellular network CPE and fixed wireless telephones**" and, in Phase 6, "consumer-facing connected devices and IoT infrastructure, with products including **IoT gateways**" — [CAICT GMA: India — TEC publishes MTCTE Phase 6 compliance requirements](https://gma.caict.ac.cn/en/update/119/845); [CAICT GMA: Certification Updates of TEC](https://gma.caict.ac.cn/en/update/119)
- Phases III and IV were announced earlier — [TÜV Rheinland: India TEC Update — MTCTE Phase 3 and Phase 4 Announced](https://www.tuv.com/regulations-and-standards/en/india-tec-update-mtcte-phase-3-and-phase-4-announced.html)
- Authoritative product list: [TEC MTCTE Phase-wise Products List](https://www.mtcte.tec.gov.in/phaseWiseproductsList) *(blocked by proxy — not read)*; [TEC: MTCTE landing page](https://www.tec.gov.in/mandatory-testing-and-certification-of-telecom-equipments-mtcte); mirror listing at [UMS PCS: MTCTE Phase Wise Products List](https://umspcs.in/service/mtcte-phase-wise-products/)
- There are documented **non-applicability / exemption provisions** for certain test parameters for "IP Multimedia Conferencing Equipment, LAN Switch & IoT Gateway", and exemptions "pertaining to WiFi CPE and IP Router" — [CAICT GMA](https://gma.caict.ac.cn/en/update/119/845)
- Consultancy overviews: [SEYECS: TEC Approval & MTCTE Certification Complete Guide India 2026](https://blogs.seyecs.com/tec-approval/); [SEYECS: TEC Certification India](https://seyecs.com/services/product-compliance/tec/)

### Inferences
- **The distinction to give the founder, plainly:**
  - **WPC ETA (DoT / Wireless Planning & Coordination)** = *spectrum* permission. "Are you allowed to transmit on this frequency in India?" Self-declaration, ₹10,000, days. Applies to **anything with a radio**, including this product.
  - **TEC MTCTE (Telecommunication Engineering Centre)** = *telecom network equipment* conformance. "Does this piece of network infrastructure meet Indian telecom technical requirements?" Full lab testing against TEC Essential Requirements, materially more expensive and slower. Applies only to **enumerated telecom equipment categories**.
  - **They are independent.** A product can need both (a Wi-Fi router: ETA for the radio + MTCTE as a WiFi CPE/router), one (this luminaire: ETA only), or neither.
- **Modulark's exposure: ETA yes, MTCTE almost certainly no.** The luminaire and the sensor node are endpoints that join someone else's Wi-Fi network. They are not gateways, not CPE, not routers, not switches. The risk to watch is if the product roadmap ever adds a **hub/bridge/gateway** device that terminates a WAN or bridges protocols — *that* device could land in MTCTE scope and would be a materially more expensive certification. Design the roadmap knowing this.
- Because the primary phase list could not be read, this conclusion is an **inference from category descriptions**, not a verified absence. Confidence is high but not absolute.

### Gaps
- **The MTCTE phase-wise product list was not read directly** (mtcte.tec.gov.in blocked). Before launch, open that list and confirm no entry matches "smart lighting", "wireless sensor", "short range device" or similar. This is a 10-minute check and should be done.
- Phase VII (if announced) was not checked — the phases have been rolling out roughly annually and a 2026 phase may exist.

## Cross-cutting summary for the report writer

### Takeaway
The compliance picture resolves into one strategic choice: **how much mains voltage the company is willing to own.** Every expensive obligation — CRS luminaire registration, the severe enclosure flammability regime, the effective ban on 3D-printed housings — attaches to the *mains* path. The radio is cheap and easy. The battery is the one unavoidable serious cost.

### Inferences
**The likely obligation set, ranked by cost and certainty:**

| Item | Regime | Likely status | Indicative cost | Time |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Lithium battery pack | BIS CRS, IS 16046 (Part 2):2018 | **Mandatory, unavoidable** | ₹15k BIS + testing (destructive, likely upper end of ₹10–30k) + consultant | 2–3 months |
| Mains luminaire (if fixed) | BIS CRS, IS 10322 (Part 5/Sec 1):2026 | Mandatory **if** product form is a fixed luminaire | ₹30k–50k+ per registration | 6–9 weeks |

5. Ask a BIS-recognised lab directly whether they will test an additively-manufactured enclosure and on what basis. *(Cheap, and converts the biggest inference in these notes into a fact.)*
