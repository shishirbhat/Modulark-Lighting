## (A) E-waste and battery EPR obligations

### Takeaway
A consumer LED luminaire is squarely inside Schedule I of the E-Waste (Management) Rules, 2022, and there is **no turnover or volume threshold that exempts a small producer** — the obligation attaches to the act of placing EEE on the market, not to size. Because the product ships with a lithium pack and swap packs are sold separately, the company is simultaneously a "producer" under the **Battery Waste Management Rules, 2022**, which is a *separate* registration, separate target, separate return and separate environmental compensation regime. This dual-EPR exposure is the single most commonly missed obligation for hardware startups in this category.

### Cited Findings

**Coverage and scope**
- The E-Waste (Management) Rules, 2022 were notified by MoEFCC and are applicable from **1 April 2023** — [Saikrishna & Associates](https://www.saikrishnaassociates.com/moefcc-notifies-e-waste-management-rules-2022/); [Wealthy Waste](https://www.wealthywaste.com/the-e-waste-management-rules-2022-applicability-on-use-import-of-used-equipment-and-dismantling-of-electrical-and-electronic-equipment-used-in-industrial-processing)
- The 2016 rules covered 21–22 product categories, mostly IT and select consumer appliances; the **2022 update expanded coverage to over 130 items/product categories**, adding solar panels, medical devices, toys and — relevantly — lighting — [Clarity UPSC summary of the Rules](https://clarityupsc.com/environment-upsc-notes/e-waste-management-rules-recycling-india-upsc); [Lexology — Navigating India's E-Waste Regulation](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=e85300c9-e1fb-4745-b007-b94eefa97005)
- Schedule I contains an entry for **"other lighting or equipment for the purpose of spreading or controlling light, excluding filament bulbs"**, with an average life assigned of **3 years for LED used by consumers** and **5 years for LED used for professional purposes** — [CPCB draft average-life document / search summary](https://cpcb.nic.in/openpdffile.php?id=UmVwb3J0RmlsZXMvMTUwOV8xNjc5Mzc0NjE5X21lZGlhcGhvdG8xMDQ1Mi5wZGY%3D); [Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=e85300c9-e1fb-4745-b007-b94eefa97005)
- EPR is defined in the Rules as the responsibility of any producer of EEE **as given in Schedule I** for meeting recycling targets **as per Schedule III and Schedule IV**, discharged **only through registered recyclers** — [Gem Recycling](https://gemrecycling.com/what-is-epr-how-to-calculate-epr-targets-for-e-waste/)
- CPCB estimates e-waste generation using **sales data of registered producers × average life of the equipment** from Schedule I — [Gem Recycling](https://gemrecycling.com/what-is-epr-how-to-calculate-epr-targets-for-e-waste/)

**EPR target schedule (escalating percentages)**
- For established producers, EPR obligations are effective from 1 April 2023 and are a percentage **of the quantity of waste generation** (i.e. of sales made one average-life ago, not current sales):
  - **FY 2023-24 and FY 2024-25: 60%**
  - **FY 2025-26 and FY 2026-27: 70%**
  - **FY 2027-28 onwards: 80%**
  — [Gem Enviro / Gem Recycling](https://gemrecycling.com/what-is-epr-how-to-calculate-epr-targets-for-e-waste/); [National Recycling Corporation 2026 guide](https://www.nationalrecycling.in/blog/e-waste-management-rules-india-2026-compliance-guide/); [Respose India on the 80% target](https://www.resposeindia.com/post/80-percent-e-waste-epr-target-recyclers)
- **New producers with no sales history are NOT exempt — they fall under Schedule IV**, which sets targets against recent sales rather than against a life-adjusted waste-generation estimate:
  - **FY 2023-24: 15% of sales made in FY 2021-22**
  - **FY 2024-25: 20% of sales made in FY 2022-23**
  - **FY 2025-26 onwards: 20% of the sales figure of two financial years prior**
  — [Gem Recycling](https://gemrecycling.com/what-is-epr-how-to-calculate-epr-targets-for-e-waste/); [CPCB FAQ (PDF, via search summary)](https://cpcb.nic.in/uploads/Projects/E-Waste/FAQ_ewaste_23012024.pdf)
- Note the practical consequence for a brand-new entity: in its first two years of sale the "sales two years prior" figure is **zero**, so the quantified recycling target is nil — **but the registration, return-filing and RoHS obligations still bite from day one**, and the target ramps to 20% of year-N-2 sales thereafter. (See Inferences.)

**No small-producer exemption**
- No turnover, revenue or unit-volume de minimis threshold was found in any source. Every source describes the obligation as attaching to **"all producers, importers, brand owners and manufacturers of electrical and electronic equipment"** without qualification — [Green Permits / Seyecs 2026 EPR guide](https://blogs.seyecs.com/epr-registration-for-e-waste/); [About EPR — e-waste](https://aboutepr.com/e-waste)
- The user's suspicion is therefore **confirmed on the evidence available**: there is no small-producer carve-out analogous to the GST ₹40 lakh threshold. A company selling its first hundred units is a "producer" with the same registration and RoHS duties as a multinational. (Flagged as a high-confidence but not primary-text-verified negative — see Gaps.)

**Registration, returns and the portal**
- Producers register on the **CPCB EPR portal** and obtain an EPR registration number; the portal is the single national route — [CPCB EPR portals index](https://cpcb.nic.in/all-epr-portals-of-cpcb/); [SORT Consultancy step-by-step](https://sortconsultancy.com/blogs/cpcb-epr-portal-registration-guide)
- **Quarterly returns** must be filed on the CPCB portal detailing waste generated, waste handed to recyclers, and recycling certificates received; plus an **annual return**. From **FY 2025-26 onwards all quarterly and annual EPR returns must be filed digitally on the CPCB portal — physical submissions are no longer accepted** — [AVI Group, EPR E-Waste Annual Return FY 2025-26 checklist](https://avigroup.in/2026/03/18/epr-e-waste-annual-return-fy-2025-26-complete-compliance-checklist/)
- Compliance is discharged by **buying EPR certificates from CPCB-registered recyclers**, generated by those recyclers from verified end-product recovery; certificates are **kilogram-denominated**, **valid for roughly two years**, and **traded through the CPCB electronic trading platform** (platform guidelines issued **January 2025**) — [GreenSutra, E-Waste EPR Explained 2026](https://greensutra.in/news/e-waste-epr-explained-2026/)

**Costs, certificate prices and Environmental Compensation**
- **Registration fee:** for a new business with recently started sales, CPCB charges a **flat ₹10,000 for first registration**; the fee is otherwise set on a **slab basis tied to the annual recycling-target quantity** and is annually renewable. Recyclers and refurbishers pay separate, lower fees — [GreenSutra](https://greensutra.in/news/e-waste-epr-explained-2026/); [Green Permits — EPR registration process, documents & fees](https://www.greenpermits.in/10/epr-registration-for-e-waste-process-documents-fees/)
- **Certificate price band:** the **E-Waste (Management) Amendment Rules, 2024** banded the certificate exchange price between **30% and 100% of the environmental compensation** payable for non-fulfilment under Rule 22 — so the rupee floor moves with whatever EC rate CPCB has fixed, rather than being a number written into the Rules — [GreenSutra](https://greensutra.in/news/e-waste-epr-explained-2026/)
- Under CPCB's 2024 guidelines, minimum and maximum certificate prices were set across **seven product categories**; for consumer electronics (TVs, refrigerators) this worked out to **between ₹22 and ₹74 per kilogram** — [GreenSutra](https://greensutra.in/news/e-waste-epr-explained-2026/)
- **Environmental Compensation formula:** EC = **shortfall (kg) × handling/processing cost rate determined by CPCB**. CPCB's **final EC guidelines under the e-waste rules were issued on 25 August 2025** — [GreenSutra](https://greensutra.in/news/e-waste-epr-explained-2026/); [CPCB EC Guidelines PDF (link, not fetchable here)](https://eprewaste.cpcb.gov.in/assets/PDF/EC-Guidelines-under-E-Waste-Management-Rules-2022-25.08.25.pdf.pdf); see also [Jeenali, Environmental Compensation in India's E-Waste Rules](https://jeenaliewaste.com/blogs/environmental-compensation-e-waste-india/)
- **Litigation overhang on the price band:** large manufacturers (air-conditioning and appliance majors) challenged the floor/ceiling band before the **Delhi High Court** from late 2024; the Centre defended it; hearings ran through 2025; a **January 2026 interim order stayed enforcement against one petitioner**. **No final judgment had issued as of June 2026**, and the band otherwise remains operative — so compliance budgeting should price certificates **at the floor** — [GreenSutra](https://greensutra.in/news/e-waste-epr-explained-2026/); background: [Newslaundry, April 2025](https://www.newslaundry.com/2025/04/15/a-corporate-mutiny-threatens-indias-e-wonderland-dream)

**Battery Waste Management Rules, 2022 — the second, separate regime**
- The BWM Rules 2022 cover **all battery types — portable, automotive, industrial and EV**, explicitly **including Li-ion** — [IEA policy database](https://www.iea.org/policies/25166-battery-waste-management-rules-2022); [AZB Partners](https://www.azbpartners.com/bank/battery-waste-management-rules-2022-a-new-era-for-battery-recycling/)
- Per **Rule 4(1)**, producers have EPR for batteries they **introduce in the market**; they must ensure waste batteries are recycled through **certified waste-battery recyclers** and must **obtain EPR certificates from those recyclers** in proportion to quantity recycled — [CPCB Battery EPR FAQ](https://eprbattery.cpcb.gov.in/upload/adminDoc/Frequently%20Asked%20Questions%20(General).pdf); [CPCB Battery EPR SOP](https://eprbattery.cpcb.gov.in/upload/adminDoc/SOP.pdf)
- Coverage explicitly extends to **devices containing batteries and spare battery packs** — i.e. selling a luminaire with an embedded/swappable pack, and selling spare swap packs, both trigger producer status — [Lawrbit, Producers' EPR registration under BWM 2022](https://www.lawrbit.com/ehs/producers-epr-registration-battery-waste-management-2022/); [Green Permits, Battery Waste EPR Compliance 2026](https://www.greenpermits.in/03/battery-waste-epr-compliance-2026-cpcb-registration-guide/)
- Registration is via a **separate CPCB portal**: `eprbattery.cpcb.gov.in` / `www.eprbatterycpcb.in` — [EPR Battery portal](https://eprbattery.cpcb.gov.in/); [CPCB notice on the battery EPR portal](https://cpcb.nic.in/openpdffile.php?id=UmVwb3J0RmlsZXMvMTUxMF8xNjc5MzkzMzc1X21lZGlhcGhvdG8yNDc1OC5wZGY%3D)
- **Material recovery targets** escalate: **70% by 2024-25, 80% by 2026 (FY 2025-26), 90% from 2026-27 onwards**; for **EV and portable batteries, 90% recovery by 2026-27** — [Drishti IAS summary of the Rules](https://www.drishtiias.com/daily-updates/daily-news-analysis/battery-waste-management-and-handling-rules-2022); [IEA](https://www.iea.org/policies/25166-battery-waste-management-rules-2022)
- **Landfill and incineration of collected waste batteries is prohibited** — [IEA](https://www.iea.org/policies/25166-battery-waste-management-rules-2022)
- **Environmental compensation** applies for missed targets, calculated on **the cost of handling, collection and transportation of waste batteries plus the processing cost for recovery of battery materials** — [IEA](https://www.iea.org/policies/25166-battery-waste-management-rules-2022)
- **Battery EPR filing fee** reported at **₹2,500** government fee, rising with the recycling target — [Metacorp, CPCB Battery EPR compliances](https://www.metacorp.in/environment-compliance/battery-epr-compliance). *(Single commercial source; treat as indicative only.)*

**RoHS (Chapter V of the E-Waste Rules)**
- **Rule 16** provides for reduction in the use of hazardous substances in the manufacture of EEE — [CPCB FAQ (search summary)](https://cpcb.nic.in/uploads/Projects/E-Waste/FAQ_ewaste_23012024.pdf); [C-MET RoHS services](https://cmet.gov.in/rohs-services)
- Substance limits: **Lead, Mercury, Hexavalent Chromium, PBB and PBDE ≤ 0.1% by weight in homogeneous materials; Cadmium ≤ 0.01% by weight in homogeneous materials** — applied to the equipment **and to its components, consumables, parts and spares** — [CPCB FAQ summary](https://cpcb.nic.in/uploads/Projects/E-Waste/FAQ_ewaste_23012024.pdf); [C-MET](https://cmet.gov.in/rohs-services)
- **Compliance is on a self-declaration basis by the producer**, but **CPCB is empowered to conduct random sampling of products placed on the market to verify RoHS compliance** — [CPCB FAQ summary](https://cpcb.nic.in/uploads/Projects/E-Waste/FAQ_ewaste_23012024.pdf)
- **C-MET (Centre for Materials for Electronics Technology)** is a designated government laboratory offering RoHS testing services in India — [C-MET](https://cmet.gov.in/rohs-services)

### Inferences
- Because the imported PCBAs are the highest-risk RoHS component and are made by a third party overseas, the self-declaration is only as good as the supplier's evidence. The practical control is to require **per-homogeneous-material RoHS test reports (XRF screening plus wet-chemistry for the ambiguous materials) from the overseas assembler at PO stage**, keep them on file, and be able to produce them if CPCB samples the product. A self-declaration unsupported by supplier test reports is the audit failure mode.
- The Schedule I entry wording found ("other lighting or equipment for the purpose of spreading or controlling light, excluding filament bulbs", consumer LED average life 3 years) fits the product exactly. The **3-year average life** matters arithmetically: for an established producer the waste-generation base is sales from ~3 years ago, so the first years of a new venture have a small base — but under **Schedule IV** the base is explicitly **sales two years prior**, which is the controlling rule for this company.
- The product almost certainly generates **two EPR registrations, two sets of targets, two certificate purchase streams and two return-filing calendars** (e-waste for the luminaire/sensor/driver; battery for the packs). Budget and calendar should be built on that assumption. Selling **spare swap packs as a standalone SKU** is the clearest possible trigger for battery-producer status, since it is a battery placed on the market as a battery.
- Total first-year EPR cash cost for a low-volume producer is likely dominated by **fees and consultant time, not certificate purchases** (because Schedule IV targets on near-zero prior-year sales are small). Indicative: ₹10,000 e-waste registration + ~₹2,500 battery EPR + consultant fees. Certificate cost scales later at roughly the ₹22/kg floor for consumer electronics.
- **EPR registration is also an import gating item in practice**: an importer of EEE is a "producer", and customs/compliance checks on EEE imports increasingly look for EPR registration. The company imports PCBAs (components) rather than finished EEE, which is a distinguishable position — but the finished product it places on the Indian market is unambiguously EEE.

## (B) Legal Metrology labelling

### Takeaway
Every retail pack of the product is a "pre-packaged commodity" and must carry the full Rule 6 declaration set; the company will need **Rule 27 registration with the Director/Controller of Legal Metrology (fee ₹500)** in its capacity as manufacturer/packer, and separately as importer if it ever imports anything in pre-packed retail form. Two waves of amendments — **Legal Metrology (Packaged Commodities) Second Amendment Rules 2025 (effective 1 February 2026)**, **Amendment Rules 2026 (notified 13 February 2026, effective 1 July 2026)** and **Second Amendment Rules 2026 (notified 27 April 2026, effective 1 July 2027)** — have tightened e-commerce and country-of-origin obligations, so any label or listing designed from pre-2025 guidance is already stale.

### Cited Findings

**Registration (Rule 27)**
- **Rule 27** requires **every individual, firm, HUF, society, company or corporation which pre-packs or imports any commodity** for sale, distribution or delivery to apply — **accompanied by a fee of ₹500** — to the **Director or the Controller** of Legal Metrology for registration of its name and complete address — [Diligence Certification on Rule 27](https://www.diligencecertification.com/rule-27-legal-metrology-act-2009/); [LegalRaasta](https://www.legalraasta.com/blog/rule-27-legal-metrology/); [Section 27, LMPC Rules 2011 (Indian Kanoon)](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/156451497/)
- Timing: application within **90 days from the date on which the applicant commences pre-packing or importing** any commodity (or within 90 days of commencement of the Rules, for pre-existing businesses) — [Diligence Certification](https://www.diligencecertification.com/rule-27-legal-metrology-act-2009/)
- The registration is described as covering the role of **"manufacturer / packer / importer"** and is obtained from the Director of Legal Metrology (central) or the state Controller depending on the applicant's footprint — [Metrify India](https://metrifyindia.com/regulatory-update/food/wholesale-packages/manufacturer-packer-registration-as-defined-in-legal-metrology-law/); [Lawyers Club India — Registration as Packer/Importer](https://www.lawyersclubindia.com/articles/legal-metrology-registration-as-packer-importer--8561.asp); [Legal Metrology India — Importer registration process](https://legalmetrologyindia.com/blog/importer-registration-under-legal-metrology/)

**Font height and placement (Rule 7)**
- **Rule 7(3):** the height of any **letter** in a declaration shall **not be less than 1 mm**; where the declaration is **blown, formed, moulded, embossed or perforated**, the height shall **not be less than 2 mm** — [TaxGuru FAQs on LMPC Rules](https://taxguru.in/corporate-law/faqs-legal-metrology-packaged-commodities-rules-2011.html); [PFNDAI note on label legibility and font size](https://pfndai.org/Document/Association_News/Regulatory_Affairs_Committee_Meeting_2019/Label_Legibility_and_Font_Size_-_Dr_JI_Lewis.pdf)
- The height of the **numerals of the net-quantity declaration on the principal display panel** is governed by **Table I** where net quantity is declared in **weight or volume**, and by **Table II** where declared in **length, area or number** — [TaxGuru FAQs](https://taxguru.in/corporate-law/faqs-legal-metrology-packaged-commodities-rules-2011.html); [custada.in reproduction of the display-panel rule](https://www.custada.in/document/document/LABELNG_MARKG_PCR.htm)
- Worked examples found: for a **~75 cm² principal display panel (100 mm × 75 mm)** the numeral height must be **at least 2 mm**; for a **~150 cm² panel (150 mm × 100 mm)** the minimum is **4 mm**; overall the numeral height scale runs **1 mm to 6 mm** depending on panel area — [NKG Advisory, Principal Display Panel](https://nkgabc.com/principal-display-panel-how-to-declare-information-on-a-package/); [Legal Culinary analysis of the Principal Display Panel](https://legalculinary.com/blogs.php?url=4.+ANALYSIS+OF+PRINCIPAL+DISPLAY+PANEL-+UNDER+LEGAL+METROLOGY+AND+FOOD+LAWS)

**Mandatory declarations (Rule 6)**
- The Rule 6 declaration set as described across sources comprises: **name and address of the manufacturer / packer / importer**; **common or generic name of the commodity**; **net quantity** in standard units; **month and year of manufacture / pre-packing / import**; **retail sale price**; **consumer care details** (name, address, phone and/or email of the person who can be contacted for complaints); and, **for imported goods, the country of origin** — [TaxGuru FAQs](https://taxguru.in/corporate-law/faqs-legal-metrology-packaged-commodities-rules-2011.html); [iPleaders on the LMPC Rules](https://blog.ipleaders.in/legal-metrology-packaged-commodities-rules-2011/); [Artwork Flow, Legal Metrology compliance guide](https://www.artworkflowhq.com/resources/legal-metrology-compliance-for-packaging-teams)
- Retail sale price is declared as the **maximum retail price inclusive of all taxes**, conventionally in the form *"Maximum Retail Price Rs ___ inclusive of all taxes"* — [Artwork Flow](https://www.artworkflowhq.com/resources/legal-metrology-compliance-for-packaging-teams); [Buy TSC Printers, Legal Metrology & MRP label requirements 2026](https://buytscprinters.com/blogs/news/legal-metrology-mrp-label-requirements-packaged-food-india-2026)

**Penalties**
- **Section 36(1), Legal Metrology Act 2009** — manufacturing, packing, selling, distributing or importing a **non-standard package**: fine up to **₹25,000** for the first offence; up to **₹50,000** for the second; for subsequent offences **not less than ₹50,000 and up to ₹1,00,000, or imprisonment up to one year, or both** — [Lexology, India: The Legal Metrology Laws](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=d1573be9-6f96-4d97-ad56-fc7e7a192d31); [MBG Corp, Legal Metrology Act 2009 compliance guide 2026](https://www.mbgcorp.com/in/insights/compliance-guide-navigating-the-legal-metrology-act-2009-for-businesses-in-india/)
- **Section 36(2)** provides separate punishment for manufacturing, packing or importing a pre-packaged commodity **with an error in net quantity** beyond the prescribed limits — [Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=d1573be9-6f96-4d97-ad56-fc7e7a192d31)
- **Contravention of Rules 27 to 31** (i.e. failing to register as manufacturer/packer/importer) is punishable with a fine of **₹4,000** — [Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=d1573be9-6f96-4d97-ad56-fc7e7a192d31)
- The Legal Metrology Act also provides for **compounding of offences**, which is how most first-instance label defects are resolved in practice — [MBG Corp](https://www.mbgcorp.com/in/insights/compliance-guide-navigating-the-legal-metrology-act-2009-for-businesses-in-india/)

### Inferences
- The ₹4,000 fine for non-registration is trivial; the ₹25,000–₹1,00,000 exposure for non-standard packages is not, and it is **per offence**, which inspectors in practice count per SKU and sometimes per consignment. For a low-volume premium product, a single enforcement visit can exceed a year's gross margin on the affected SKU.
- Legal Metrology is **the most frequently enforced of the four regimes in this note**, because state Legal Metrology inspectors physically visit retail premises and warehouses and because online listings are now openly scraped. It is also the cheapest to get right. This is the classic "small manufacturer gets caught" obligation.
- The **country-of-origin question is genuinely subtle for this company**: the finished product is assembled in India from an imported PCBA. It is not an "imported product" for Rule 6 purposes at retail — so the mandatory declaration should be the Indian manufacturer's details, not a foreign country of origin. But if the company ever imports a **finished, pre-packed retail unit** (e.g. a pilot batch fully assembled overseas, or spare battery packs in retail packaging), that pack becomes an imported pre-packaged commodity, requiring **importer registration under Rule 27** and **country-of-origin declaration**, plus (from 1 July 2026) the **Rule 6(10A) searchable/sortable country-of-origin filter** on any e-commerce listing. Founders routinely miss this second leg.
- The swappable battery pack sold as a spare is a **separate pre-packaged commodity** with its own label obligations — including its own net quantity, MRP and consumer care declaration. It cannot ride on the parent product's label.

## (C) Importing assembled PCBs — classification, duty, and attaching compliance

### Takeaway
A **populated** board is **not** classifiable under heading **8534** (which covers **bare** printed circuits); Section XVI Note 2 forces it to be classified either as the goods it constitutes in its own right or as a part of the machine it is solely/principally for. For an LED-driver + MCU + radio board destined for a luminaire the realistic candidates are **9405 99 (parts of luminaires)**, **8537** (boards/panels for electric control or distribution) and **8543 70** (electrical machines/apparatus having individual functions, n.e.s.). There is **no authoritative answer available from open sources**, the duty differential is large, and this is exactly the kind of question that should go to a **CAAR advance ruling** rather than be guessed.

### Inferences
- **Section XVI Note 2 logic, applied to this board:** Note 2(a) says parts which are *goods included in any of the headings of Chapter 84 or 85* are classified in their own heading. Note 2(b) says other parts, if suitable for use solely or principally with a particular machine, go with that machine. The complication here is that the luminaire is **Chapter 94, not Section XVI**, so Section XVI Note 2 does not directly route the board to 9405 — Chapter 94 has its own Note structure, and 9405 99 covers "parts". The practical fork is: (i) if the board is *solely and principally* a part of the luminaire and is not a good of its own heading, **9405 99** is arguable; (ii) if the board is itself a good of heading 8537 (a panel for electric control) or 8543 (apparatus with an individual function), those headings take precedence over the "parts" heading. Given the board combines an **LED driver + MCU + radio**, a customs officer could plausibly land on **8543 70** (individual function, n.e.s.) or on **8537** (control). **This is genuinely contestable and is not resolvable from open sources.**
- **Why getting it wrong matters, concretely:** (a) the BCD differential across 9405 99 / 8537 / 8543 / 8517 is material and compounds through SWS and IGST; (b) a wrong heading can pull in or wrongly claim a **notification-based concession**, and CAAR's PCBA ruling shows concessions are read strictly and conditionally; (c) misclassification found on post-clearance audit triggers **differential duty plus interest plus penalty**, and where the department alleges wilfulness, the extended limitation period; (d) **the heading determines whether CHIMS applies** (only 8542 codes) and interacts with BIS/CRS and WPC scope. The cheap insurance is a **CAAR advance ruling** before the first commercial consignment — binding on the department for that applicant and product.
- **A defensible default** (to be tested, not assumed) is that a board that is **dedicated to and shipped for** a specific luminaire model, with no independent commercial function, is best declared as a **part of a luminaire under 9405 99**, with the classification rationale documented in a technical write-up filed with the Bill of Entry. The inclusion of a **radio module** is what most weakens that position and pushes toward 8517 or 8543.
- **BIS/CRS applies to the finished luminaire the company sells in India, not to the imported board.** The company is the *manufacturer* for BIS purposes and must hold its own CRS registration under IS 10322 before it can lawfully sell — and the guidelines were revised in 2026, so the applicable standard version must be checked. Because the enclosures are 3D-printed in-house, thermal and insulation testing against IS 10322 is a real technical risk, not a paperwork exercise: printed polymers behave differently from moulded ones in glow-wire and temperature-rise tests.
- **WPC ETA is a hard customs gate with confiscation as the stated remedy.** Because the radio is inside an imported PCBA, the sensible sequence is: source a **pre-certified radio module** whose ETA can be relied on, or obtain ETA for the board/finished product before the first import. ETA cannot be retrofitted at the port.
- For a first-time importer with a small consignment, a **Customs Broker (CHA) is effectively necessary** — ICEGATE filing, classification declaration, and the BIS/WPC document set are not realistically self-served on a first shipment. No sourced cost or timeline figure was obtained (see Gaps).

## (D) GST rate and invoicing

### Takeaway
GST was restructured with effect from **22 September 2025** (56th GST Council; **Notification No. 9/2025-Central Tax (Rate) dated 17 September 2025**, superseding Notification 1/2017-CTR): the **12% and 28% slabs were removed**, leaving principally **5% and 18%** (plus a 40% demerit rate). **LED lighting appears to have moved from 12% to 5%**, and **all batteries under 8507 are now a uniform 18%**. **However, sources conflict on whether HSN 9405 sits at 5% or 18%, and this conflict is not resolved by the evidence available — it must be checked against the notification text before the company sets an MRP.** The kit structure (luminaire at one rate + battery at 18% + sensor) is a live **composite vs mixed supply** problem under Section 8 CGST.

**Composite vs mixed supply (Section 8 CGST Act)**
- **Composite supply**: two or more goods/services **naturally bundled and supplied together in the ordinary course of business**, where **one is a principal supply**. **The rate applicable to the principal supply applies to the whole bundle** — [TaxGuru, Composite supply and mixed supply under Section 8](https://taxguru.in/goods-and-service-tax/composite-supply-mixed-supply-gst-section-8-cgst-act-2017.html); [GST Gyaan on Section 8](https://gstgyaan.com/section-8-gst-liability-on-composite-and-mixed-supplies)
- **Mixed supply**: two or more supplies **made together for a single price**, where each **can be supplied separately and is not dependent on the others** — [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/goods-and-service-tax/gst-composite-mixed-supplies-cgst-act-2017.html); [e-Startup India](https://e-startupindia.com/learn/composite-supply-vs-mixed-supply-under-gst/)
- **Section 8(b): a mixed supply is taxed at the rate of that particular supply which attracts the HIGHEST rate of tax.** Canonical example: a gift hamper of chocolates (12%), soft toy (18%) and perfume (28%) is taxed entirely at **28%** — [TaxGuru, Section 8 of GST Act](https://taxguru.in/goods-and-service-tax/section-8-gst-act-tax-liability-composite-mixed-supplies.html); [FinTax Blog on Section 8](https://fintaxblog.com/section-8-of-cgst-act-2017-tax-liability-on-composite-and-mixed-supplies/)

- **E-way bill:** **₹50,000 general threshold for inter-state movement across India**; intra-state thresholds depend on the state/UT notification, though **most states follow ₹50,000** — [Bajaj Finserv, e-way bill limit](https://www.bajajfinserv.in/eway-bill-limit); [Busy.in, state-wise e-way bill thresholds 2026](https://busy.in/gst/state-wise-threshold-limits-for-e-way-bills/); [The India Post, e-way bill limit and rules](https://indiapost.org/e-way-bill-limit-and-rules). West Bengal cut its intra-state threshold to ₹50,000 — [KNN India](https://knnindia.co.in/news/newsdetails/state/west-bengal/west-bengal-govt-cuts-e-way-bill-threshold-to-rs-50000-for-intra-state-goods-movement)
- **GST registration threshold for goods:** **₹40 lakh** aggregate turnover in normal-category states; **₹20 lakh** in special-category states. For services it is ₹20 lakh / ₹10 lakh — [Tax2win](https://tax2win.in/guide/gst-registration-limit); [ClearTax, GST registration threshold limits](https://cleartax.in/s/gst-registration-limits-increased); [Razorpay](https://razorpay.com/learn/gst-registration-limits/); [Tax Garden, ₹40 lakh vs ₹20 lakh 2026](https://taxgarden.in/blog/gst-registration-turnover-limit-40-lakh-20-lakh-threshold-india-2026)

### Inferences
- **The kit is the trap.** A boxed product containing a wall disc, a lithium pack, an LED head and a sensor node, sold for a single price, is on its face a **mixed supply** unless the components are **naturally bundled with one identifiable principal supply**. If it is a mixed supply, **Section 8(b) applies the highest rate in the box**. With the battery at a confirmed **18%** and the luminaire plausibly at **5%**, treating the kit as a 5% supply would **understate GST on the entire kit value by 13 percentage points** — a liability that compounds silently with interest and penalty until an audit.
- **The defensible composite-supply argument**, if the company wants the luminaire rate: the pack, head and sensor are **not usable independently of the wall disc**, the kit is **only ever sold as a working luminaire**, the marketing and the warranty treat it as one product, and the **principal supply is the luminaire**. That is a real argument. It is materially **weakened** by the fact that the company also sells **spare swap packs as a standalone SKU** — which demonstrates the battery *can* be supplied separately, the exact criterion the mixed-supply definition turns on.
- **The clean structural fix** is to decide deliberately rather than by default: either (i) present and invoice the kit as one luminaire SKU with a single HSN and a documented composite-supply rationale on file, accepting that the standalone spare-pack SKU must be invoiced separately at 18%; or (ii) line-item the invoice with each component at its own rate — which is **not permitted for a genuine composite or mixed supply** and only works if the components are genuinely separately supplied at separately agreed prices. Option (iii), the conservative one, is to **charge 18% on the whole kit**, which is always safe on the tax side and costs margin. An **advance ruling under Section 97 CGST** is the definitive answer and is cheap relative to the exposure.
- **The spare swap pack sold alone is unambiguously 8507 at 18%.** There is no argument otherwise.
- **The sensor node**, if sold bundled, follows the kit analysis; if sold alone, its heading (8517 vs 9027/9031 vs 8543) drives its rate, and no rate was obtained for those headings.
- **Registration is effectively compulsory from day one** for this business regardless of turnover: it makes **inter-state B2C sales** (which forces registration under the inter-state rule), and without registration it **cannot claim IGST paid on imported PCBAs as input tax credit** — which for an import-dependent BOM is a large working-capital item. **IGST paid at import is creditable** to a registered importer; it is a cost, not a tax, only if unregistered.
- **ITC on 3D printers** (capital goods) is available to a registered person using them in the course or furtherance of business, subject to the standard conditions and to the rule that depreciation must not be claimed under the Income-tax Act on the tax component. *(This is the general position; no specific source was captured — see Gaps.)*
- **E-invoicing will not apply** at low volume (₹5 crore AATO), but **e-way bills will** — a single consignment of finished goods or an inbound import movement above ₹50,000 needs one, and a small manufacturer shipping direct-to-consumer via courier is exactly the profile that forgets this.
- At sub-₹5-crore turnover the company needs only **4-digit HSN on B2B invoices** and **no HSN at all on pure B2C invoices** — but should nevertheless use full HSN throughout, because the HSN is what evidences the rate position on the kit.

## Cross-cutting: obligations small manufacturers most commonly get caught by

1. **Dual EPR.** Registering for e-waste EPR and assuming that covers the battery. It does not — battery EPR is a separate registration on a separate portal with separate targets and returns, and **selling spare swap packs makes it unavoidable**.
2. **No small-producer exemption for EPR.** Unlike GST's ₹40 lakh threshold, EPR attaches on the first unit sold. (Confirmed absence of any threshold across sources; flagged for primary verification.)
3. **BIS/CRS for the finished luminaire.** Founders think of BIS as an import issue. It is a **manufacturing and selling** issue — registration under IS 10322 is required before lawful sale, and the **IS 10322:2026 revision** means old guidance is stale. 3D-printed enclosures add genuine test risk.
4. **WPC ETA before the first import, not after.** Radio equipment imported without WPC permission is **confiscated at the port**.
5. **Legal Metrology on the spare-pack SKU.** Every separately sold pack is its own pre-packaged commodity needing its own full declaration set.
6. **The composite/mixed supply rate on the kit.** Defaulting to the luminaire rate on a box containing an 18% battery is a 13-point silent liability if the kit is a mixed supply.
7. **Classification of the imported PCBA.** Guessing between 9405 99 / 8537 / 8543 / 8517 rather than obtaining a CAAR advance ruling.
8. **Rule 27 Legal Metrology registration** — ₹500, 90-day deadline from commencement of pre-packing, and almost universally overlooked until an inspector asks.
9. **E-way bills below the e-invoicing threshold.** Being too small for e-invoicing does not exempt ₹50,000+ consignments from e-way bills.
10. **RoHS self-declaration without supplier test reports.** Self-declaration is accepted at filing but CPCB samples products from the market; the imported PCBA is the exposure.
